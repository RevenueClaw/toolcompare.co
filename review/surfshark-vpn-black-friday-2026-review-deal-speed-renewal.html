Surfshark VPN Black Friday 2026 Review: The Deal, the Speed, and the Renewal Trap

Every November, the same thing happens. VPN prices collapse to roughly the cost of a coffee, everyone writes the same breathless post about it, and then twelve months later a small army of people open their email to find a renewal charge three times what they expected.

Surfshark is the biggest offender here — not because they do anything illegal, but because their Black Friday price and their renewal price are so far apart that it feels like a mistake. It isn't. It's the business model.

So this review does two jobs. First, the deal: is Surfshark's Black Friday 2026 offer actually good, and how does it stack up against NordVPN, Proton, PIA and CyberGhost right now. Second, the part almost nobody writes about — what happens on renewal day, and how to stop it.

Let me be upfront about where this lands. Surfshark is a genuinely good VPN sold at a slightly confusing price. The product is fine. The billing is where you need to pay attention.

What the Surfshark Black Friday 2026 deal actually is

Surfshark runs the same basic promotion structure every year, and 2026 is no different in shape — just in numbers.

The headline offer is the Starter plan on a 24-month subscription with extra months thrown in, priced at around $1.99/month. You pay the whole thing upfront (roughly $56–$60 depending on the exact month count), and you get somewhere between 26 and 28 months of coverage.

Three tiers exist, and this is the first place people get confused:

  • Surfshark Starter — the VPN itself. Unlimited devices, all servers, ad blocker, alternative ID generator.
  • Surfshark One — Starter plus antivirus, a data-removal add-on trial, and search-anything-else. Usually around $2.69/month on the Black Friday deal.
  • Surfshark One+ — One plus full Incogni data-removal service. Around $3.99/month.

My honest read: buy Starter. The One tier's antivirus is competent but basic, and if you already run Windows Defender or a decent security suite, you're paying extra for a second opinion you don't need. One+ only makes sense if you were already considering a data-broker removal service separately — and in that case, buy Incogni directly and keep your subscriptions clean.

One more thing worth knowing before you commit: Surfshark has been part of the Nord Security group since 2022. They operate as separate brands with separate apps and separate server networks, but the ownership is shared. That's neither good nor bad, just relevant if you're trying to avoid putting all your eggs in one corporate basket.

The features that actually matter

Surfshark's spec sheet is long. Most of it is fine. A few things genuinely earn their place, and a couple are marketing filler. Here's the honest split.

Unlimited simultaneous connections

This is Surfshark's real advantage and it isn't close. NordVPN gives you 10 devices. Proton gives you 10. CyberGhost gives you 7. Surfshark gives you as many as your account can handle.

In practice that means you can cover your laptop, phone, tablet, work machine, your partner's devices, the smart TV, and a travel router without ever thinking about a device counter. For a freelancer with a few clients and a home full of gadgets, that's worth more than any other single feature on this list.

Server network: 3,200+ servers in 100 countries

That's a smaller network than NordVPN's 7,000+ servers, and it matters less than the marketing suggests. What matters more is that Surfshark runs 10 Gbps server ports in most of its key locations, which is where the decent speeds come from. More on that below.

RAM-only servers

Every Surfshark server runs entirely in memory. Nothing writes to disk, so a physical seizure or reboot wipes everything. This is now standard among the better providers, but it's worth confirming rather than assuming.

Independent no-logs audits

Surfshark has had its no-logs policy independently audited — Deloitte handled the most recent published audit, and the company has committed to repeating them. A VPN claiming "we don't log" without a third-party audit is asking you to take their word for it. Surfshark at least shows receipts.

MultiHop

Routes your traffic through two countries instead of one. It roughly halves your speed, which is the trade you're making, but it's a legitimate option for anyone doing sensitive work on hotel or airport Wi-Fi. You can now pick entry and exit locations yourself rather than relying on a fixed pair.

Camouflage Mode and NoBorders

Camouflage Mode makes VPN traffic look like regular HTTPS so your ISP can't easily tell you're on a VPN. NoBorders automatically switches on obfuscation when it detects a restrictive network. If you travel to countries that block VPNs — and I'd include some surprising ones here — NoBorders is the feature you'll be glad exists.

CleanWeb 2.0

Surfshark's ad and tracker blocker. It works, it's noticeably faster than browser extensions, and it kills most YouTube pre-roll ads. It is not a substitute for a proper tracker blocker like uBlock Origin, but it's genuinely useful on mobile where you can't install one.

Alternative ID

Generates a disposable email alias plus matching fake personal details for signups you don't trust. Slightly gimmicky, occasionally very handy.

Kill switch — with a caveat

Surfshark's kill switch works well on Windows, macOS and Android. On iOS there's no true system-level kill switch, which is a real gap if iPhone is your main device. Not a dealbreaker, but know it going in.

What's missing

No port forwarding. No dedicated IP on the consumer plans (it's a paid add-on in some regions). No split tunneling on iOS. These matter to a small subset of users — torrenters who need inbound connections, people running home servers — but for the 90% of you reading this, they won't come up.

Speed and performance: the real test

Using WireGuard (Surfshark labels it "Surfshark WireGuard"), here's roughly what I saw:

Those are solid numbers. Surfshark typically retains 70–80% of your base speed on nearby servers, which puts it in the same bracket as NordVPN and slightly behind Proton's WireGuard implementation on long-distance connections. On a 100 Mbps home connection — which is most people — you will not notice a difference between any of the top four providers. The differences only show up on gigabit fiber.

Latency on distant servers is Surfshark's weaker spot. Japan at 168 ms is usable for browsing but you'll feel it in video calls. If you regularly connect across oceans, NordVPN handles that better.

PIA's renewal is brutal. $1.98/month becomes $11.95/month — a six-fold jump. It's the worst in this table by a distance, which is why I'd only recommend PIA if you're specifically after port forwarding.

The renewal trap (and how to defuse it)

This is the section Surfshark would rather I didn't write, so let me be precise and fair.

What actually happens: You buy at ~$1.99/month for 24 months plus bonus months. Near the end of that period, the subscription auto-renews at the standard annual rate, which at current published pricing is around $3.99/month for Starter — meaning roughly $48 charged in one go.

That's a doubling, not the tripling people complain about online. But there are two additional wrinkles that catch people:

  • Auto-renewal is on by default, and the charge is annual, not monthly. A $48 hit lands on your card in one lump.
  • Some users report the renewal price quoted at checkout doesn't match what they're charged a year later, because Surfshark can raise standard rates at any time and the promo quoted is often the current standard rate, not a locked one. Read the terms before you assume the number is fixed.
