You’ve probably used Canva’s free tier to whip up a social post or a flyer. It’s great—until you hit that “this element is only available in Pro” wall. For small business owners and freelancers, that moment triggers a familiar question: Should I finally upgrade to Canva Pro?

I’ve been using Canva Pro daily for the past six months—designing everything from client presentations to Instagram carousels. I’ve also compared it side-by-side with the free version and with competitors like Adobe Express, Visme, and Pixlr. In this Canva Pro review, I’ll break down exactly what you get, who it’s actually for, and whether the $13/month price tag pays for itself.

What Is Canva Pro?

Canva Pro is the paid subscription tier of Canva, the popular drag-and-drop design platform. It unlocks a massive library of premium assets, advanced editing tools, and team collaboration features that the free plan restricts.

Think of it this way: the free plan gives you a solid toolkit and thousands of templates. Pro removes the watermarks, adds millions of stock photos, videos, and graphics, and gives you time-saving features like Background Remover, Magic Resize, and Brand Kits.

Canva Pro is not a separate app—it’s the same web-based editor (and desktop/mobile apps) with a VIP pass to everything behind the paywall.

Who Is Canva Pro For?

Canva Pro is designed for anyone who creates visual content regularly—especially if you’re doing it for a business or client. Here’s who gets the most value:

  • Freelancers – designers, social media managers, content creators who need quick, professional-looking graphics.
  • Small business owners – entrepreneurs who wear the marketing hat and need to produce consistent branding across channels.
  • Marketing teams – even small teams benefit from shared brand kits, templates, and approval workflows.
  • Non-designers – people who don’t have Adobe skills but still need polished visuals.

If you’re a casual user making one birthday invite a month, stick with the free plan. But if you’re creating multiple designs per week and are frustrated by the free limitations, Pro quickly becomes worth it.

Canva Pro Features: A Deep Dive

Let’s get into the specifics. I’ve tested every major Pro feature—here’s what actually works and what’s overhyped.

1. Background Remover

This is the killer feature for many. One click and Canva removes the background from any image. It’s not perfect—complex hair edges can look jaggy—but it’s surprisingly accurate for 90% of cases. I use it for product photos and profile pictures. The free plan offers it as a 7-day trial; after that, you need Pro.

2. Magic Resize

Need to turn a square Instagram post into a landscape Facebook cover or a vertical Story? Magic Resize duplicates your design and automatically adjusts the dimensions. You can resize up to 10 formats at once. It saves me about 15 minutes per campaign.

3. Brand Kit

Pro lets you create multiple brand kits. Each kit stores your brand colors, fonts, logos, and even pre-approved templates. If you manage more than one business or client, this is a lifesaver. The free plan only gives you one brand kit (and limited fonts).

4. Premium Stock Assets

Canva Pro unlocks over 100 million stock photos, videos, audio tracks, and graphics—all royalty-free. In my testing, the quality rivals Shutterstock or Unsplash. The search is decent, though sometimes you’ll find the same photo repeated in different categories.

5. Magic Write (AI Text Generator)

Canva’s built-in AI assistant can generate copy for your designs. Type a prompt like “funny Instagram caption for a coffee shop” and it spits out options. It’s useful for quick drafts, but the output can be generic. You’re better off using Jasper (affiliate link) for serious copywriting.

6. AI-Powered Editing Tools

  • Magic Eraser – removes unwanted objects from photos (like a simplified Photoshop clone).
  • Magic Edit – select an area and describe what you want to replace it with (e.g., “a sunset sky”).
  • Text to Image – generates images from prompts. Quality varies—good for abstract backgrounds, not for specific product shots.

7. Schedule Social Media Content

Pro includes a native scheduler for posting directly to Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, and Pinterest. It’s basic—no analytics or optimal posting times—but handy if you don’t want to use a separate tool like Buffer.

8. Team Collaboration

You can invite team members, assign roles (viewer, editor, admin), and even set approval workflows. The free plan allows sharing a link, but Pro gives you real-time collaboration with version history.

9. Folders & Storage

Free users get 5GB of cloud storage. Pro gives you 100GB. That matters if you save lots of designs or upload your own images.

10. Custom Templates

You can create templates for your team or clients and lock certain elements (e.g., logo, colors) to prevent accidental changes.

What We Liked About Canva Pro

  • Ease of use – The learning curve is almost zero. If you’ve used the free version, Pro feels like unlocking a secret level.
  • Background Remover – Saves hours compared to manually masking in Photoshop.
  • Brand Kits – Perfect for agencies or anyone with multiple brands.
  • Magic Resize – Huge time-saver for repurposing content across platforms.
  • Value for money – At $13/month (billed annually), it’s cheaper than a single stock photo subscription.
  • Regular updates – Canva keeps adding AI features; the product evolves fast.

What Could Be Better

  • AI features are hit-or-miss – Magic Write and Text to Image are decent but not best-in-class. For serious AI image generation, you’re better off with Midjourney or DALL·E.
  • No offline mode – You need an internet connection to use Canva (except the mobile app for basic editing).
  • Export options limited – You can export to PNG, JPG, PDF, SVG, and GIF. No PSD or AI export. Also, no CMYK for print—so professional printers may give you trouble.
  • Advanced vector editing is weak – If you need precise bezier curves or complex vector shapes, stick with Illustrator.
  • Social scheduler is basic – No analytics, no best-time recommendations, no multi-image posts for Instagram carousels.
  • Customer support – Email only for Pro users (no live chat). Response times can be 24–48 hours.

Canva Pro Alternatives

Not sure if Canva Pro is right for you? Here are the top alternatives in 2026:

Adobe Express (formerly Adobe Spark)

  • Price: Free / Premium $9.99/month
  • Best for: Users who already have Adobe Creative Cloud
  • Pros: Tighter integration with Photoshop & Illustrator, more advanced photo editing
  • Cons: Fewer templates, less intuitive UI than Canva
  • Verdict: Good if you need Adobe ecosystem; otherwise Canva is more user-friendly.

Visme

  • Price: Free / Starter $12.25/month / Pro $24.75/month
  • Best for: Data visualization and presentations
  • Pros: Excellent charts, infographics, and slide decks
  • Cons: Steeper learning curve, mobile app is weak
  • Verdict: Better for reports and presentations than social graphics.

Pixlr

  • Price: Free / Premium $7.99/month
  • Best for: Quick photo editing without subscription fatigue
  • Pros: Cheap, great photo filters, AI tools
  • Cons: No templates for social media, limited collaboration
  • Verdict: A photo editor, not a design suite.

Adobe InDesign (for print)

  • Price: $22.99/month (single app)
  • Best for: Professional print layouts (magazines, brochures)
  • Pros: Industry standard for print, precise typography
  • Cons: Expensive, steep learning curve, no web-based version
  • Verdict: Overkill for 95% of Canva users.

Microsoft Designer

  • Price: Free / Pro $9.99/month
  • Best for: Microsoft 365 users
  • Pros: Integrates with Word, PowerPoint; AI-powered
  • Cons: Smaller template library, less mature
  • Verdict: Worth trying if you live in Microsoft’s ecosystem.

Verdict: Is Canva Pro Worth It?

If you’re creating designs for your business at least a few times a week, yes, Canva Pro is absolutely worth the $13/month. The time saved by Background Remover and Magic Resize alone justifies the cost. Add in the stock photo library (which would cost $30+/month elsewhere) and Brand Kits, and it’s a no-brainer.

Skip Pro if:

  • You only design occasionally (birthday invites, personal projects).
  • You need professional print output (CMYK, bleed marks).
  • You’re a professional graphic designer who needs vector precision.

Buy Pro if:

  • You’re a freelancer or small business owner who needs fast, consistent designs.
  • You manage multiple brands or clients.
  • You want to stop paying for separate stock photo subscriptions.

Our Pick: Canva Pro is the best value design tool for non-designers. It’s not perfect—the AI features are still maturing, and the social scheduler is basic—but for the price, nothing else comes close in ease of use and asset library.
