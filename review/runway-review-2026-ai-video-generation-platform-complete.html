I've been testing AI video tools since 2023. Back then, generating a coherent clip of a cat walking felt like magic. Now? Runway has evolved into something far more capable — and far more confusing for newcomers.

Runway started as a research lab focused on multimodal AI. They built popular tools like the text-to-video generator Gen-1 back in 2023, then Gen-2, and now Gen-3 and Gen-4 systems. But is this the AI video generator you should actually pay for in 2026? Let's find out.

I spent two weeks running real projects through Runway: a 30-second brand ad, a short explainer, and some creative experiments. Here's everything I learned.

What Exactly is Runway?

Runway is a cloud-based AI video creation platform. You can generate video from text prompts, images, or existing footage. The platform also includes tools for editing, removing backgrounds, adding effects, and applying motion tracking — all without a dedicated GPU or video editing experience.

Think of it as a Swiss Army knife for video, except some of the blades still need sharpening.

Who actually uses Runway?

  • Content creators and YouTubers
  • Small marketing teams
  • Freelance video editors
  • Agencies prototyping concepts
  • Filmmakers exploring AI in pre-production

Core Features Deep Dive

1. Text-to-Video (Gen-3 and Gen-4)

This is the main event. Type a prompt like "a woman walking through a rainy city street at night, cinematic lighting, slow motion" and Runway generates a clip.

Gen-3 (the current standard): Good for short clips (5-10 seconds). Handles motion reasonably well. Faces can still look a bit plastic, and hands sometimes morph into eldritch tentacles. Prompt-following is solid for basic scenes.

Gen-4 (beta, early access): Slightly better at motion coherence, slower generation times. Characters appear more consistent between shots. Still rough around the edges — I tried prompting "a chef slicing vegetables" and got a kitchen that shifted between scenes like a fever dream.

My take: Text-to-video is good for mood boards, concept tests, and background footage. It's not ready for final client deliverables unless your client has very low standards.

2. Image-to-Video

Upload a static image and turn it into a short animation. This is genuinely useful. I uploaded a product photo — a minimalist chair — and turned it into a 360-degree rotating video in about 90 seconds.

Results depend heavily on your source image. Clean, well-lit images with clear subjects work best. Busy photos with many objects produce chaotic results.

3. Video-to-Video (Style Transfer)

Take existing footage and apply a new visual style. Think painting filters, claymation, or anime aesthetic. Ran some test footage of a dog running through grass and applied "watercolor illustration." It looked… interesting. Not polished, but actually kind of artistic.

Best use case: Creative projects where stylization hides imperfections. Don't expect photorealism from style transfer.

4. Inpainting and Outpainting

  • Inpainting: Select a specific area in a video and regenerate it. Remove an object, change a texture, fix weird AI artifacts.
  • Outpainting: Extend the frame beyond its original boundaries.

The outpainting tool works surprisingly well for expanding backgrounds. I had a tight close-up of a person and expanded the scene to show a full room. The AI filled in details that looked mostly coherent.

Warning: Inpainting can take 3-5 minutes per edit. Not a tool for fast iteration.

5. Green Screen and Background Removal

Clean background removal without a physical green screen. Upload your video, click a button, and Runway detects the subject. Works impressively well for simple scenes — talking head videos, product shots.

Fail cases: Hair, glass, fast moving objects. Anytime there's transparency or fine details, expect manual cleanup.

6. Motion Brush

Select an area of an image and tell it how you want that area to move. Paint across a river and type "flowing water." Paint clouds and type "drifting slowly."

This is one of the most accessible features for beginners. You don't need to write complex prompts — just point and type. Results are decent but lack precision. You can't control exact motion paths.

7. Frame Interpolation (Slow Motion)

Turn 24fps footage into 60fps, or slow down clips smoothly. Upload standard video, Runway adds artificially generated frames.

Compared to tools like Topaz Video AI, Runway's interpolation is good but not exceptional. Fine for social media. For professional slow-motion, you'll want dedicated software.

What Runway Does Well

  • Accessibility for non-editors: If you've never touched Premiere Pro or After Effects, Runway's interface is refreshingly simple.
  • Cloud processing: Your laptop doesn't need a gaming GPU. Everything runs on their servers.
  • Regular updates: The team pushes new features frequently. Motion Brush and Gen-4 were both added within months of each other.
  • Batch processing: Generate multiple clips with similar prompts. Useful for asset libraries.
  • Community templates: Users share prompt libraries and styles. Good starting point if you're stuck.
