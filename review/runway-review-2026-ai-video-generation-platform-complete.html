Runway Review 2026: AI Video Generation Platform Complete Walkthrough

Runway has been at this longer than almost anyone. The company started building AI video tools back in 2018, years before "text to video" became a phrase people typed into Google. That head start shows up in the product — not always in raw clip quality, but in the sheer number of things you can do to a clip once it exists.

I've spent the last several months using Runway on actual paid work: two product spots for a DTC brand, a batch of social cutdowns, and one short film that we'll get to later. I've run the same prompts through Sora, Veo, and Kling to see where Runway wins and where it doesn't.

Short version: Runway is the best AI video platform for people who need to edit and control footage. It is not the cheapest way to generate a pretty 8-second clip, and its credit system will punish you if you don't understand it before you subscribe.

Here's the full breakdown.

What Runway actually is in 2026

Runway isn't one model. It's a suite of models and tools wrapped in a browser-based editor, and the model lineup changes every few months. As of this review, the important pieces are:

  • Gen-4 — the flagship text-to-video and image-to-video model. Higher fidelity, better prompt adherence, slower and more expensive.
  • Gen-4 Turbo — the fast, cheaper sibling. Roughly 5 credits per second versus 12 for full Gen-4. Quality drop is noticeable on complex motion, less so on talking heads and product shots.
  • Gen-4 References — lets you feed the model reference images of a character, object, or location and keep it consistent across shots. This is the feature that made Runway viable for narrative work.
  • Gen-4.5 / newer releases — Runway ships model updates constantly. If you're reading this a few months after publication, check the model dropdown before assuming anything below still applies.

Around those models sits a pile of supporting tools: video inpainting, background removal, motion tracking, camera control, lip sync, text-to-speech, voice cloning, frame interpolation, and upscaling. Plus Aleph, Runway's in-context video editing model, which lets you modify existing footage with a text prompt — add a rainstorm, swap a jacket color, relight a scene.

That combination is the actual pitch. Anyone can generate a clip now. Very few platforms let you generate a clip and then fix it.

Text-to-video and image-to-video

Both work. Image-to-video is where Runway earns its money — give it a still and it animates it with reasonable physics. For product work, this is the workflow I use most: shoot or generate a hero still, then let Runway add motion.
