Notion has become the ultimate digital playground for teams and solo creators. It’s a wiki, a project board, a database, a document editor—all held together by blocks. Then in 2023, they added an AI layer. By 2026, it’s matured, but the question remains: does it actually Make your work faster, or is it just another feature you barely use?

I’ve been living inside Notion AI for the past three months—writing, summarizing, querying, and automating. I also ran it alongside other AI tools like ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot to see where it truly shines (and where it falls short). This review covers everything: features, pricing, integration, limitations, and the honest verdict on whether your team should pay the extra $10 per member per month.

What is Notion AI, Anyway?

Notion AI is an add‑on that gives the standard Notion workspace generative AI capabilities. It can write, edit, summarize, translate, answer questions about your notes, and automate some database tasks. It’s not a separate app—you access it by hitting the spacebar or highlighting text inside any Notion page.

The core idea: instead of jumping to ChatGPT, pasting context, and copying back, you stay inside your workspace. The AI has access to the pages you give it permission to see (your chosen workspace or specific pages). This context‑awareness is supposed to save you time and keep workflows contained.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

1. AI Writing Assistant

This is the most common use. You can:

  • Draft from scratch – Type a prompt like “write a project kickoff email to the client” and get a full draft.
  • Continue writing – Highlight a few sentences and ask the AI to expand.
  • Summarize – Condense a long page or database view into key points.
  • Improve writing – Fix grammar, change tone, shorten, or make it more professional.
  • Translate – Supports dozens of languages, though accuracy varies for less common languages.

Real‑world example: I maintain a monthly newsletter inside Notion. With AI, I can take my loose bullet‑point notes and turn them into a 500‑word editorial draft in about 20 seconds. The tone is decent—sometimes a bit generic—but it gives me a strong starting point.

Limitations: The AI doesn’t understand your personal brand voice out of the box. You’ll need to re‑prompt or edit aggressively if you want something that doesn’t sound like a bland corporate blog. Also, it hallucinates facts—I asked it to summarize a meeting note it had access to, and it invented a decision that never happened.

2. Q&A (Ask Anything)

This is Notion AI’s superpower. You can type a question in natural language, and it searches across all pages you have access to—then gives you a short answer with citation links.

Example: I asked “What’s our current Q3 marketing budget?” The AI pulled data from three database entries and a meeting note, then gave me a number. It linked each source so I could verify.

Strengths: Great for teams with lots of documentation. No more digging through hundreds of pages. It respects permissions—you only see answers from pages you’re allowed to see.

Weaknesses: If your data is inconsistent or poorly structured, the answers can be wrong. It also struggles with ambiguous names. One test: “Show me all tasks assigned to Sarah” returned tasks from two different Sarahs with no way to refine. You can rephrase, but it’s clunky.

3. AI Properties & Database Automation

Notion databases are powerful, but repetitive. AI can:

  • Auto‑fill properties – For example, generate a summary of a project description into a “Short Summary” text property.
  • Generate formulas – Type “calculate total = price * quantity” and it writes the formula for you.
  • Summarize rows – Create a rollup property that summarizes all child pages in a database.

Practical use: I have a content calendar database. When I create a new page for a blog post, I can set AI to auto‑generate three meta tags and a one‑line summary based on the draft. Saves about 10 seconds per post—but across 50 posts, that adds up.

Drawback: This only works on text‑heavy properties. You can’t automatically categorize by sentiment or extract structured data like prices from paragraphs.

4. Integration with Existing Notion Workflow

Everything feels native. Highlight text → a floating menu pops up with AI options. Hit space → a prompt box appears. You can also use slash commands like /ai.

You can choose to let AI have access to your entire workspace or only specific pages (per‑workspace setting). That’s crucial for privacy—you don’t want sensitive financial data being sent to OpenAI’s servers without control (which happens when you use ChatGPT directly).

But the AI currently doesn’t work on mobile apps as smoothly. On iOS, you can generate text, but the context window is smaller and typing prompts is slow. Tablets are better, but still not as good as desktop.

5. Limits and Missing Features

Notion AI does not generate images, analyze uploaded PDFs (beyond text extraction), or handle multi‑step workflows. You can’t say “create a database view that shows projects overdue by more than a week” and have it execute. It’s a text helper, not an automation engine.

Also, it only works in English for Q&A. Translate works for other languages, but the main writing assistant is heavily English‑optimized. If your team works in Spanish or German, expect more errors.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Fast drafting and summarizing – Saves real time if you write often.
  • Context‑aware Q&A – Pulls answers from your own knowledge base without switching apps.
  • Native integration – No copy‑paste; works with blocks, databases, and permissions.
  • Privacy controls – You can limit AI access to specific pages.
  • Good value for existing Notion users – $10/member/month is cheaper than a dedicated AI assistant.

Cons

  • Hallucinates – Even with context, it invents facts confidently. Always double‑check.
  • No image generation – You still need a separate image tool.
  • Mobile experience is poor – Not useful on phones.
  • Only English for Q&A – Multilingual support is limited.
  • Slow for large databases – Querying a 5000‑row database can take 10+ seconds.
  • Not an automation engine – Can’t execute complex multi‑step commands.

Final Verdict: Is Notion AI Worth It?

Yes, if you are a heavy Notion user—someone who lives in the app daily for project management, documentation, and writing. The time savings on drafting, summarizing, and searching your notes are real. I calculated I save about 45 minutes a week using AI writing alone. For a team of five, that’s roughly $20 per month per saved hour—which makes the $50/month total (5 members × $10) a net win.

No, if you use Notion only occasionally or if your work requires multimodal AI (images, web search, code execution). In that case, a dedicated ChatGPT subscription makes more sense because you aren’t paying for a base Notion plan you don’t fully use.

The clear winner for its specific niche: Notion AI is the best integrated AI workspace tool for teams that already rely on Notion. It’s not a universal AI assistant—it’s a productivity enhancer tailored to your existing notes and databases. For everyone else, stick with general‑purpose AI.

FAQ

1. Can I use Notion AI without a Notion subscription?

No. You must have at least the free Notion plan, but the free plan only allows a 30‑day trial of AI. After that, you need a paid plan (Plus or higher) plus the $10 add‑on.

2. Does Notion AI use my data to train its models?

Not by default. You can control this in workspace settings under “AI & data.” Notion does not use your content to improve OpenAI models unless you explicitly opt in (they recommend keeping it off for privacy).

3. What languages does Notion AI support for Q&A?

Q&A works best in English. Translate and text generation support many languages (Spanish, French, German, Japanese, etc.), but accuracy drops significantly. For non‑English workspaces, you’ll likely encounter more hallucinations.

4. Can I turn off Notion AI for specific team members?

Yes, in the workspace admin settings. You can enable AI on a per‑member basis. This is useful if only certain roles (writers, project managers) need it.

5. Does Notion AI work offline?

No. It requires an internet connection. The AI processing happens on remote servers (OpenAI infrastructure), so you can’t use it on a plane without Wi‑Fi.

6. How does Notion AI compare to Microsoft Copilot in Teams?

Copilot integrates with Microsoft 365 apps (Word, Teams, SharePoint). If your company already lives in the Microsoft ecosystem, Copilot may offer deeper integration with Outlook, Excel, and meetings. Notion AI is better if your workflow is centered around Notion databases and wiki‑style documentation. There’s room for both.

7. Will Notion AI replace the need for a separate writing assistant like Grammarly?

No. Grammarly focuses on real‑time grammar, tone, and style corrections across any app. Notion AI can help rewrite existing text, but it doesn’t catch typos as you type. The two complement each other.
