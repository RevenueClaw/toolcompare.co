Let's be real for a second: Notion was already a solid workspace tool before the AI hype train arrived. You could build wikis, track projects, and manage databases—all in one place. Then in 2023, they added AI features, and I'll admit, I was skeptical.

Fast forward to 2026, and Notion AI has matured into something I actually use daily. But is it worth the extra $10/month? Or are you better off sticking with the free version and pairing it with something like ChatGPT?

I've spent the last three months testing Notion AI across real workflows—writing blog posts, summarizing meeting notes, managing a content calendar, and even drafting code snippets. Here's the unvarnished truth.

What Is Notion AI?

Notion AI is a set of generative AI features baked directly into the Notion workspace. Unlike slapping a chatbot on top of your notes, these tools are integrated at the paragraph, page, and database levels.

You can:

  • Generate text from prompts
  • Summarize long pages or meeting notes
  • Fix spelling and grammar
  • Translate content
  • Create action items from project updates
  • Write formulas for databases (this one's huge)
  • Brainstorm ideas without leaving your workspace

The key difference from just using ChatGPT: context. Notion AI knows what page you're on, what your database contains, and can work within your existing structure.

Real-World Feature Breakdown

1. AI Writing Assistant

This is the feature most people will use daily. Inside any page, hit the slash command /AI and you get options: draft a blog post, write a pros/cons list, generate a to-do list, or continue writing from where you left off.

Does it work? Yes, but with caveats.

For short-form writing—emails, meeting agendas, quick notes—it's surprisingly good. I wrote a project update email in about 15 seconds. The tone was professional without being robotic.

For long-form content like this review? Not so much. The AI tends to be generic and repetitive beyond 500 words. You'll get better results by using it to draft outlines or opening paragraphs, then editing heavily.

One trick I found: Use the "Continue writing" feature to expand bullet points into full sentences. That workflow actually saves me about 30% of writing time.

2. Summarization

This is where Notion AI genuinely shines. Paste in a 3,000-word meeting transcript or a long research article, and the AI will spit out a bullet-point summary that actually captures the key points.

I tested it against a 40-minute recorded meeting note (about 5,000 words). The summary was 8 bullet points. I missed one detail about a deadline change, but everything else was dead accurate.

For anyone drowning in meeting notes or research docs, this alone might justify the subscription.

3. Database Formulas (The Hidden Gem)

If you use Notion databases for tracking projects, clients, or inventory, you know formula writing is a nightmare. The syntax is obtuse.

Notion AI can now generate formulas from natural language. Type "calculate days remaining until due date" and it writes the formula. Works about 80% of the time. When it doesn't, adjusting the prompt usually fixes it.

This feature alone saved me on a client project with a complex inventory tracker.

4. Q&A With Your Workspace

This is the feature that still makes me nervous. You can ask Notion AI questions about your workspace: "What tasks are due this week?" or "Show me the budget for Q3."

It works, but the accuracy depends entirely on how organized your workspace is. If you're messy (like me), you'll get partial results. If you have strict naming conventions and proper tagging, it's basically magic.

5. Translations and Grammar

Solid but unspectacular. Translation handles 30+ languages adequately for internal use (don't use it for client-facing documents). The grammar checker is fine but no substitute for Grammarly in my testing.

Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • Deep integration means you never leave your workspace
  • Summarization genuinely saves time on long documents
  • Formula generation is a lifesaver for database users
  • Drafting and brainstorming works well for short content
  • Privacy is better than public chatbots (data stays in your workspace)
  • The 20 free responses let you test before paying

Cons:

  • Writing quality degrades noticeably beyond 300-400 words
  • No image generation or multimodal support (unlike ChatGPT)
  • The $10/month per person adds up quickly for teams

Final Verdict: Is Notion AI Worth It?

Here's my honest take after three months.

Buy Notion AI if:

  • You already use Notion daily for work or projects
  • You process lots of meeting notes, research, or long documents
  • You struggle with Notion database formulas
  • The $10/month won't hurt your budget
  • You want AI that knows your workspace context

Skip it if:

  • You rarely use Notion
  • You mostly need AI for long-form writing
  • You're on a tight budget and can use ChatGPT for free
  • Your workspace is disorganized (fix that first)
  • You need multimodal features (images, voice, etc.)

The verdict: Notion AI is a good product in search of the right user. For power users who live in Notion, it's a genuine productivity boost. For casual users, it's an expensive extra that you'll forget you're paying for.

If I had to pick one winner between Notion AI and ChatGPT Plus for someone starting fresh? I'd say ChatGPT Plus offers better value for most people. But for the Notion-obsessed among us—and I count myself in that group—Notion AI is worth the monthly fee.

Start with the 20 free responses. Use them for summarization and formula writing. If those features click for you, upgrade. If not, your wallet will thank you.

FAQ

Does Notion AI use your data for training?

Notion states that data in private workspaces is not used to train their AI models. Enterprise customers get additional data protection guarantees. You can disable AI features per workspace if privacy is a concern.

Can I use Notion AI offline?

Limited. Some cached AI features work offline, but most require an internet connection. The mobile app is particularly dependent on connectivity.

Is Notion AI available in all languages?

The interface is in English, but it can generate and translate content in 30+ languages including Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese.

How many responses can I get per day with Notion AI?

There's no daily limit, but very heavy usage (hundreds of prompts per day) may trigger a temporary cooldown. Most users won't hit this.

Does Notion AI work on mobile?

Yes, but the experience is limited. The slash command works in the mobile app, and summarization is available. Voice input is not supported, which feels like a missed opportunity.

Can I cancel Notion AI mid-month?

Yes, and you keep access until your billing period ends. There are no long-term contracts.

Does Notion AI replace the need for a writing assistant like Grammarly?

Not entirely. Notion AI's grammar checker is decent, but Grammarly is still better for real-time grammar, style suggestions, and browser-wide support. If you write a lot in multiple apps, keep Grammarly.

Will Notion AI get cheaper in the future?

Hard to say. The $10/month add-on has been stable since launch, and Notion hasn't announced any price changes for 2026. They do offer discounts for annual billing (about two months free).
