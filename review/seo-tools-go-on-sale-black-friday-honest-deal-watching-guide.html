Do SEO Tools Go on Sale for Black Friday? An Honest Deal-Watching Guide (Moz, 2026)

Every November, someone in a marketing Slack channel asks the same question: is it worth waiting for Black Friday to buy SEO software? The answer is more annoying than a simple yes or no — it depends entirely on which tool you're eyeing, and Moz sits right in the middle of that spectrum.

Here's the short version before we get into the weeds. Moz does run Black Friday promotions most years, but they're rarely dramatic. You're typically looking at 20–30% off annual Moz Pro plans, occasionally a bit more on Moz Local. Compare that to Semrush, which treats Black Friday like a second Christmas, or Ahrefs, which mostly ignores the whole thing and lets its pricing page do the talking.

So if you're holding a credit card and waiting for a Moz Black Friday deal in 2026, this guide is for you. We'll cover what Moz has actually offered in past years, what you get for the money, how it stacks up against the competition's November discounts, and — most importantly — whether you should buy at all.

Why Some SEO Tools Discount and Others Don't

Before we talk numbers, it helps to understand the economics, because it explains why your favorite tool does or doesn't participate.

SEO software is a subscription business with high fixed costs (crawling infrastructure, link indexes, server bills) and near-zero marginal cost per user. That means discounts are cheap to offer and expensive to get wrong. A tool with strong retention doesn't need to discount — its customers renew anyway. A tool fighting for market share, or one with a big sales team to keep busy, discounts aggressively.

That's why you see the pattern you do:

  • Semrush discounts hard and often. It's the loudest Black Friday voice in SEO, and its deals usually hit 30–40% off annual plans.
  • Ahrefs almost never discounts. When it does, it's a modest annual-plan nudge, not a headline event.
  • Moz lands in the middle. It runs a Black Friday/Cyber Monday promo most years, usually framed as a percentage off annual billing, and usually in the 20–30% range.
  • Smaller tools (Mangools, SE Ranking, Ubersuggest) go deepest, sometimes 40–60%, because that's how they win customers away from the big three.

One thing worth internalizing: a 30% discount on an overpriced tool is still an overpriced tool. Always compare the discounted price to what a competitor charges at full price. We'll do that math below.

What Moz Has Actually Offered on Black Friday

Let's be precise, because "Moz runs a Black Friday sale" gets repeated so often that people assume it's a 50% off blowout. It isn't.

Over the past several years, Moz's November promotions have followed a fairly consistent shape:

  • Discount on annual Moz Pro plans, typically 20–30% off the first year. In some years this has been positioned as an extra chunk off on top of the standard "two months free" annual discount.
  • Moz Local promotions, sometimes bundled — extra months or a percentage off per-location pricing.
  • A short window. These deals usually run from the Friday after Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday, occasionally extended a few days. They are not month-long.
  • New customers and upgrades, generally. Existing monthly subscribers usually can't retroactively apply the promo to a plan they already have — you'd typically need to upgrade to annual billing, and even then it's worth confirming with support before you pay.

The honest read: Moz's Black Friday offer is a real discount, but it's a modest one. If you were going to buy Moz Pro on annual billing anyway, waiting for the promo is free money. If you were on the fence about Moz entirely, a 25% discount probably shouldn't be the thing that tips you.

What You're Actually Buying: Moz Pro in 2026

Moz Pro is a four-part toolkit: keyword research, link analysis, site crawling, and rank tracking. It's been around since 2004, which means it's mature, stable, and — depending on who you ask — either comfortingly familiar or noticeably dated.

Keyword Explorer is the strongest piece for most users. It gives you search volume, difficulty, and a "Priority" score that blends volume, difficulty, and organic CTR into a single number. The difficulty metric is the one everyone quotes, and it's still one of the more consistent ones across tools. You also get SERP analysis showing the top 10 results with domain authority, page authority, and estimated click-through rates — genuinely useful for judging whether you can realistically rank.

Link Explorer is Moz's weakest link in the chain, and I mean that literally. The index is large but smaller than Ahrefs', and new links can take noticeably longer to show up. For a small business checking a handful of competitors, that's fine. For an agency doing link prospecting at volume, it's a real limitation. The one thing Link Explorer still owns is Domain Authority — a metric Moz invented that clients recognize, which makes it useful for reporting even if you know it's a proxy, not a truth.

Site Crawl handles technical audits: broken links, redirect chains, duplicate content, missing meta descriptions, crawl errors. It's competent. It's not Screaming Frog, and it doesn't pretend to be — you get scheduled crawls and a clean issue dashboard rather than a desktop crawler with infinite configuration.

Rank Tracking is where the plan tiers bite hardest. Tracked keyword limits are tight on the Starter plan, and if you're managing more than one client site you'll feel the ceiling fast.

Moz Local is sold separately and is genuinely good for its niche — it pushes your business listings to major directories and flags inconsistencies. At $14/month per location (or about $129/year per location), it's a reasonable line item for a multi-location local business and a waste of money for anyone else.
