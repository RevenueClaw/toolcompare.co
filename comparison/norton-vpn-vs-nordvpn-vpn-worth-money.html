Norton VPN vs NordVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

Norton VPN and NordVPN get lumped together a lot, and honestly, that's a little unfair to both of them. They're built for different buyers.

NordVPN is a standalone privacy tool made by a company that does nothing else. Norton VPN comes from Gen Digital, the same folks behind Norton 360, Avast, AVG, and LifeLock. One is a specialist. The other is a bundle.

That distinction matters more than any spec sheet, because it changes what you're actually paying for. So let's look at where each one wins, where each one disappoints, and which one deserves your card details in 2026.

This comparison draws on hands-on use of both apps, published third-party speed tests, and each company's own audit reports and privacy policies. Prices shift constantly, so treat every number below as a snapshot and verify before you check out.

The short version

If you want the 30-second answer: NordVPN wins for most people. It's faster, it's been independently audited multiple times, it works in more countries, and it costs less per month on a long plan.

Norton VPN is the better call in two specific situations: you already want a Norton security suite and the VPN comes bundled, or you need a 60-day refund window instead of 30.

Now the details.

Server network: not even close

NordVPN runs 7,000+ servers across 118 countries. Norton VPN advertises servers in roughly 30 countries and doesn't publish an exact server count.

That gap sounds dramatic, and for some people it is. If you're traveling to Thailand, Argentina, or South Africa and want a local IP, Norton might not have you covered. NordVPN almost certainly will.

But here's the honest counterpoint: most people connect to the same three or four countries over and over. If your VPN needs are "US Netflix, UK iPlayer, and a server near home for banking," 30 countries is plenty. Server count is a headline number, not a daily-use number.

Where it does bite is torrenting. NordVPN supports P2P on servers in 40+ countries. Norton VPN doesn't market itself for torrenting at all, and its server lineup is thin for it.

Winner: NordVPN, by a wide margin.
