OrionVPN vs IceVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

OrionVPN and IceVPN show up in the same search results, sit in the same price bracket, and both promise roughly the same thing: fast servers, no logs, and a monthly bill that costs less than a coffee. So why do people keep asking which one to pick?

Because the two take very different routes to get there. OrionVPN is the friendlier of the pair — a smaller network, a cleaner app, and 24/7 live chat when something breaks. IceVPN is the scrappier one: more servers, a lower long-term price, and an actual independent audit behind its no-logs claim.

We ran both for three weeks on a 1 Gbps fiber line in Chicago and on 5G mobile, tested them against streaming libraries, checked for DNS leaks, and stressed the kill switches. Here's what actually held up.

Short answer: IceVPN is the better buy for most people in 2026. OrionVPN wins if streaming and human support matter more to you than price.

How We Tested

Quick note on method, since "we tested it" gets thrown around a lot.

  • Hardware: 1 Gbps fiber connection in Chicago, plus an iPhone on 5G and a Windows 11 laptop
  • Duration: Three weeks, roughly 40 speed tests per provider at different times of day
  • Checks: DNS leak tests, WebRTC leak tests, kill switch drop tests, streaming access on Netflix US, BBC iPlayer, Disney+, and Amazon Prime Video
  • Also looked at: app install size, how long it takes to connect, how support responds, and what the refund process actually involves

No provider got a free pass. Both have real flaws.

Feature Breakdown

Privacy and Logging Policy

Both claim a no-logs policy. That claim is worth different amounts depending on who's verifying it.

IceVPN is registered in Panama and published an independent audit of its server infrastructure and logging practices in 2025. The auditor confirmed RAM-only servers, no persistent connection logs, and no IP storage. That's not a guarantee of perfection — audits cover a snapshot in time — but it's meaningfully better than a promise on a marketing page.

OrionVPN is based in Seychelles and has not commissioned a public audit as of our testing. Its servers are RAM-only, which is good, and its privacy policy is written in plain language rather than legal fog. But you're trusting the company's word. For a lot of users that's fine. For journalists or anyone handling sensitive work, it's a gap.

One thing both do right: they accept Bitcoin and Monero, so you don't have to hand over a card.

Server Network

IceVPN runs roughly 2,400 servers across 61 countries. OrionVPN runs about 1,100 servers across 42 countries.

That gap matters more than it sounds. IceVPN has servers in places OrionVPN simply doesn't — Vietnam, Nigeria, Colombia, and a handful of Eastern European locations. If you need a specific country for banking, work, or regional content, IceVPN is more likely to have it.

OrionVPN's smaller footprint isn't automatically worse, though. Fewer servers that are well-maintained often beat a sprawling network with a few tired nodes. In practice, OrionVPN's closest servers in the US and UK were consistently less congested during evening hours.

Neither is slow. If your home connection is 200 Mbps or under, you will not notice the difference. If you're on gigabit fiber and moving large files, IceVPN's edge is real.

Streaming and Torrenting

This is where the tables turn.

OrionVPN unblocked Netflix US, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, and BBC iPlayer on the first or second server we tried. That's a better hit rate than most of the big-name VPNs we've tested, and it's clearly a priority for the company.

IceVPN got us into Netflix US reliably. BBC iPlayer worked on roughly one in four attempts. Disney+ blocked us more often than not. If streaming is your main reason for buying a VPN, that's a problem.

For torrenting, IceVPN is the stronger pick. P2P is allowed on every server, port forwarding is available, and there's a multi-hop option that routes traffic through two countries. OrionVPN restricts P2P to 12 designated servers, which works fine but gives you fewer options.

Apps and Device Support

Both allow 10 simultaneous connections. Both have apps for Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, and browser extensions for Chrome and Firefox.

The difference is polish.

OrionVPN's apps are simple in a good way. One big connect button, a searchable server list, and settings that don't require a help article. The Windows app includes a kill switch, split tunneling, and a built-in ad and tracker blocker called OrionShield that genuinely cut down page clutter in our testing.

IceVPN's apps do more but feel rougher. Split tunneling works on desktop and Android but not iOS. The Linux app is command-line only — no GUI at all, which will annoy desktop Linux users. And the kill switch on macOS took two tries to engage properly in our first week of testing. It worked after that, but it's the kind of thing that makes you want to double-check.

Support

OrionVPN offers 24/7 live chat. We got a human in under two minutes on a Sunday night and had a split-tunneling question resolved in about six minutes.

IceVPN runs email and ticket support only. Our first ticket got a reply in 14 hours. The answer was accurate, but if your VPN is down and you're on a deadline, that's a long wait.

On the two-year plan, IceVPN is about $17 cheaper overall and roughly $0.70/month less. On the three-year plan it undercuts OrionVPN's best rate by more than a dollar a month.

OrionVPN's monthly plan is cheaper, which matters if you only need a VPN for a single trip or a short project. Paying $9.99 for one month beats paying $66 upfront to save money you won't recoup.

Both offer a 30-day refund window. We tested IceVPN's — the request went through the website, no chat required, and the refund hit our card in nine days. OrionVPN's process is similar, though you do have to contact support first.

OrionVPN Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Genuinely good at unblocking streaming services — the best hit rate in this comparison
  • 24/7 live chat that actually answers, and fast
  • Clean, uncluttered apps on every major platform
  • Built-in ad and tracker blocker works without breaking sites
  • Cheaper monthly plan if you don't want to commit
  • Full GUI Linux app

Cons

  • No independent audit of its no-logs claim
  • Smaller server network — 42 countries, missing several regions
  • Slower than IceVPN on long-distance connections
  • No multi-hop, no dedicated IP, no port forwarding
  • P2P restricted to 12 servers
  • 10-device limit feels tight for larger households

IceVPN Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Independent 2025 audit backs up the no-logs policy
  • Fastest of the two, especially on distant servers
  • Biggest network — 61 countries, ~2,400 servers
  • Cheapest long-term pricing, especially on the 3-year plan
  • Multi-hop, port forwarding, and dedicated IP available
  • P2P allowed everywhere
  • Panama jurisdiction with a solid privacy track record
