ExpressVPN vs OrionVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

ExpressVPN and OrionVPN turn up in the same search results, but they aren't really the same kind of product. One is a roughly $100-a-year service with a decade of independent audits behind it and apps for just about every device you own. The other is a budget option that undercuts it by two-thirds and tells you considerably less about how it actually works.

That gap doesn't automatically Make the cheap one a bad buy. Plenty of people just want to stop their ISP from logging which sites they visit, and paying $12.95 a month for that is overkill. But there's a real difference between "cheaper" and "cheap for a reason," and that's what this comparison is about.

I've spent time with both, and I'll be upfront: this is a lopsided matchup in a few important ways. OrionVPN publishes far less detail about its infrastructure than ExpressVPN does, so some of what follows rests on the company's own claims rather than anything independently verified. I'll flag that wherever it matters.

The short version

If you want the answer without the walkthrough: ExpressVPN wins for almost everyone. It's faster in real-world testing, it works with the streaming services people actually subscribe to, it has been audited by outside firms, and it runs on eight devices at once.

OrionVPN's case rests almost entirely on price. If your budget is genuinely tight and your threat model is "I don't want my ISP seeing my browsing history," it can do that job for a fraction of the cost. If you're handling anything sensitive — work files, client data, travel to restrictive countries — the savings aren't worth the unknowns.

What each service actually is

ExpressVPN

ExpressVPN launched in 2009 and has been owned by Kape Technologies since 2021. It's registered in the British Virgin Islands, which has no data-retention laws and no intelligence-sharing agreements with the US or UK. That jurisdiction choice is a big part of why privacy-focused reviewers have historically rated it well.

The company runs over 3,000 servers in 105 countries, all of them RAM-only. That means the machines hold nothing on disk — cut the power and there's nothing left to seize. ExpressVPN has had its apps audited by Cure53 and its server technology audited by KPMG, and it publishes the results.

It also owns its own protocol, Lightway, which it built to be lighter and faster than OpenVPN without giving up encryption strength. In 2025 ExpressVPN rolled out post-quantum key exchange support on Lightway, which matters if you care about "harvest now, decrypt later" scenarios.
