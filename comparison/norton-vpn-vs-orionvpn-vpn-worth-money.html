Norton VPN vs OrionVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

Most people shopping for a VPN in 2026 want three boring things: it shouldn't leak your traffic, it shouldn't tank your connection speed, and it shouldn't cost more than the streaming service you're trying to watch.

Norton VPN and OrionVPN sit at opposite ends of that shopping list. Norton is a household name attached to a massive security company. OrionVPN is a small provider that most people have never heard of, and that's exactly why it shows up in search results next to the big brands — it's cheap, and cheap gets clicks.

So which one actually deserves your card details? I spent time with both, dug through their policies, and compared what you get for the money. Here's the honest breakdown.

Short answer: For most people, Norton VPN is the safer buy — not because it's faster or flashier, but because you can verify more of what you're paying for. OrionVPN can Make sense if your budget is genuinely tight and you understand what you're giving up. More on that below.

What Norton VPN actually is

Norton VPN is the standalone version of what used to be called Norton Secure VPN. It's owned by Gen Digital, the company that also owns Avast, AVG, LifeLock, and CCleaner — a detail worth knowing, because it shapes how you should think about the brand's privacy story.

The network runs roughly 2,000 to 3,000 servers across about 30 countries. That's mid-pack. It's nowhere near the 100+ country coverage you get from the biggest independent VPNs, but it covers the locations most people actually use: the US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Japan, Australia, and a handful of others.

Apps exist for Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS, plus browser extensions for Chrome, Firefox, and Edge. The extension is a proxy rather than a full tunnel, which is fine for unblocking a site on a laptop but doesn't protect anything outside the browser.

Where Norton gets interesting is bundling. If you already pay for Norton 360, the VPN is essentially included, and buying it separately means paying twice for something you may already own.
