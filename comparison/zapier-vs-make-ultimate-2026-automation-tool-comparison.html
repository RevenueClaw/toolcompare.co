Zapier vs Make (Integromat): The Ultimate 2026 Automation Tool Comparison

So you’re trying to automate your workflows, and you’ve narrowed it down to two names: Zapier and Make (formerly Integromat).

You’ve probably heard the buzz. Zapier is the giant, the household name. Make is the scrappier underdog with more visual flair. But which one actually works better for your specific needs—and your bank account?

I’ve been building automations for years, from simple email forwarding to multi-step data pipelines. I’ve tested both platforms inside out. Here’s my honest, no-nonsense breakdown of Zapier vs Make in 2026—covering pricing, features, ease of use, and the real-world trade-offs.

Let’s get into it.

The Short Version

Zapier is the safest bet for beginners and businesses that need to connect a ton of apps quickly—especially if you're not a power user. It’s easy, reliable, and has 7,000+ app integrations. But it gets expensive fast.

Make is more affordable and way more flexible for complex, multi-branch automations. Its visual builder is powerful, but it has a steeper learning curve. If you're technical or budget-conscious, Make is a no-brainer.

Feature Breakdown

App Integrations

Zapier: Over 7,000 apps. Everything from Gmail, Slack, and Salesforce to niche CRM tools and databases. They’re constantly adding new apps. For sheer breadth, Zapier wins.

Make: Around 2,000+ apps. It covers all the major players, but you’ll miss some less common ones. However, Make makes up for it with better flexibility in custom API calls—you can connect almost anything if you can code a little.

Verdict: If you need a specific obscure app, check Zapier first. For broader coverage, Zapier has the edge.

Visual Builder & Complexity

This is where Make shines.

Zapier uses a linear, top-down “if this, then that” flow. It’s straightforward—set a trigger, add an action, done. But try to add branching logic (e.g., "if email is from X, send to Slack; otherwise, send to Trello"), and you hit a wall. You can use filters and paths, but it’s clunky and limited.

Make gives you a visual canvas. You drag and drop modules, connect them with lines, and see your entire workflow at a glance. Need loops, filters, routers, or error handling? No problem. It’s basically a mini flow chart. For complex automation, Make is way more powerful.

Verdict: Beginners love Zapier. Power users love Make.

If you’re running even moderate automation, Make will save you a ton of money.

Pros and Cons

Zapier Pros

  • Easiest to learn. Anyone can set up a Zap in minutes.
  • Massive app library. If it exists, it’s probably on Zapier.
  • Reliable. Uptime and execution are rock-solid.
  • Great support. Their help docs and community are thorough.

Zapier Cons

  • Expensive. Scales poorly for heavy usage.
  • Limited logic. Branching, loops, and error handling feel tacked on.
  • No visual canvas. You’re stuck with that linear flow.
  • Premium app limits. Lower-tier plans restrict how many “premium” apps you can use.

Make Pros

  • Incredible value. Cheaper per operation by a wide margin.
  • Powerful visual builder. Complex automations become a breeze to design.
  • Advanced logic. Loops, filters, routers, error handling—all built-in.
  • Custom API support. Connect to anything with HTTP modules.

Make Cons

  • Steeper learning curve. Newcomers can feel overwhelmed.
  • Smaller app library. You might miss a niche integration.
  • Interface feels busy. The visual canvas can get messy if you’re not careful.
  • Support is decent but not as fast as Zapier.

Which One Should You Choose?

Choose Zapier if:

  • You’re a complete non-technical beginner
  • You need a specific niche app that’s only on Zapier
  • You want to set up simple automation in under 5 minutes
  • Price isn’t your #1 concern

Choose Make if:

  • You want to save money—especially at scale
  • You’re building complex multi-step workflows
  • You’re comfortable with a visual builder
  • You want full control over error handling and branching
  • You’re a freelancer, small team, or savvy marketer

Final Verdict

If I had to pick one, it’s Make.

Why? Because it does almost everything Zapier can do, but cheaper and with more flexibility. The visual builder alone is worth the switch for anyone who’s ever wrestled with Zapier’s paths feature. If you’re willing to spend 30 minutes learning the interface—and you watch a YouTube tutorial or two—you’ll wonder why you ever paid Zapier’s premium.

That said, Zapier isn’t obsolete. Some people just want automation to work without thinking about it. And for the price of coffee, you can get started with Zapier’s free tier and stay there for a while. But for most businesses that are actually serious about scaling automation, Make is the smarter choice.

FAQ

Is Make better than Zapier?

For most use cases, yes. Make is cheaper, more flexible, and has a far superior visual builder. But Zapier is easier to learn and has a bigger app library.

Can I migrate from Zapier to Make?

Yes. You can rebuild your workflows manually in Make. There’s no direct import tool, but the process is straightforward if you understand your own logic. Make’s interface makes it easy.

Which has better customer support?

Zapier has more responsive support, especially for paid plans. Make’s support is good but can be slower, especially on free accounts.

Are Make’s free tasks enough?

Make’s free tier gives 1,000 operations per month, which is plenty for personal use or very light automation. Zapier’s free tier gives only 100 tasks.

Does Make support all Zapier integrations?

No. Make has about 2,000+ apps vs Zapier’s 7,000+. But Make allows custom API calls, so you can connect to anything with a public API.

Which is better for small businesses?

Make—by a long shot. The pricing scales gracefully, and the visual builder makes complex workflows manageable without dev help.
