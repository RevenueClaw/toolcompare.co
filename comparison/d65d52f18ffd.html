Hubspot vs Salesforce for Small Business: Which CRM Is Right for You?

Choosing a CRM for your small business feels a bit like picking between two very different kinds of Swiss Army knives. HubSpot is the shiny, all-in-one tool that promises to do everything out of the box. Salesforce is the modular beast that can be built into anything – once you invest the time (and money) to set it up.

I’ve spent the last month running both through real-world small business scenarios. I imported contacts, set up pipelines, automated emails, and even tried to break things on purpose. Here’s what I found about HubSpot vs Salesforce for small business – no fluff, just what actually works.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

1. Contact Management

HubSpot wins for simplicity. Add a contact, it automatically logs every email, meeting, and call. The activity timeline is clean – you can scroll through years of interaction without getting lost. The free plan gives you up to 1 million contacts, which is more than enough for 99% of small businesses.

Salesforce treats contacts as one half of a relationship with an account. That’s great if you sell B2B and need to manage multiple contacts per company. But it’s overkill if you’re a freelancer or a small eCommerce brand. The standard layout feels cluttered – you’ll want to customize fields and page layouts immediately.

Verdict: HubSpot for simplicity and speed. Salesforce if you need account-level hierarchies.

2. Sales Pipeline Management

Both tools let you drag deals across stages. But the experience is night and day.

HubSpot’s pipeline is visual from the start. You get a deal board, a forecast dashboard, and deal rotation (round-robin) built into the free and starter plans. Creating a new pipeline takes two clicks. You can also add custom stages and assign probabilities.

Salesforce offers more pipeline power – you can have multiple currencies, complex approval processes, and even use AI (Einstein) to predict close probabilities. But the default pipeline view (List View) is ugly. You’ll need to switch to the Kanban view or install a third-party app. And setting up any kind of advanced forecasting requires a lot of clicking.

Verdict: HubSpot for ease of use. Salesforce for enterprise-grade forecasting.

3. Marketing Automation

This is where the gap really shows.

HubSpot has native marketing automation built into its hubs. You can create email sequences, landing pages, forms, and chatbots – all within the same system. For a small business, that means you don’t have to stitch together Mailchimp + a form builder + a separate chatbot.

Salesforce’s marketing capabilities are a mess for small businesses. You need Marketing Cloud (starts around $1,250/month) for true automation, or you can use Pardot (now called Marketing Cloud Account Engagement) which is still expensive. Most small businesses on Salesforce end up using third-party tools like ActiveCampaign or HubSpot anyway.

Verdict: HubSpot wins this hands-down. Salesforce’s marketing tools are priced for enterprises.

4. Email Integration & Activity Tracking

Both connect to Gmail and Outlook, but the implementation is different.

HubSpot’s free Chrome extension tracks opens, clicks, and attachment views. You can also log emails automatically by BCCing a unique address. It’s almost effortless.

Salesforce requires the Salesforce Inbox app (starts at $25/user/month for the Essentials version, plus extra for the full Lightning Sync). Without it, email tracking is manual. The free Outlook integration only syncs contacts, not activities.

Verdict: HubSpot – you get email tracking for free. Salesforce wants you to pay more.

5. Reporting and Dashboards

HubSpot’s free plan includes basic reports (deal stage, lead source, activity). The paid Sales Hub gives you customizable dashboards, but you are limited in how many you can create. The drag-and-drop builder is easy – you don’t need to know SQL.

Salesforce’s reporting is the gold standard in the CRM world – you can build report types, cross-object reports, and complex dashboards with up to 20 components. But the learning curve is real. Expect to spend hours watching YouTube tutorials just to build a simple pipeline report.

Verdict: Salesforce if you need deep analytics. HubSpot if you need something that works out of the box.

6. Mobile App

Both offer solid mobile apps. HubSpot’s app is cleaner and faster – you can log calls, check timelines, and even use voice-to-text for notes. Salesforce’s mobile app is powerful but often feels sluggish. You can access custom objects, run reports, and use global search, but the UI is cramped on smaller screens.

Verdict: HubSpot for daily mobile use. Salesforce if your team needs to access custom objects on the go.

7. Integrations & App Ecosystem

Salesforce has the biggest marketplace in the CRM space – 7,000+ apps. If you need to connect an obscure ERP or legacy system, Salesforce probably has an integration. HubSpot’s marketplace is smaller (1,200+) but covers all the major tools: Slack, Calendly, Zapier, QuickBooks, Mailchimp, etc.

Verdict: Salesforce for breadth. HubSpot for quality – most HubSpot integrations work better out of the box.

Pros and Cons

HubSpot Pros

  • Free CRM is genuinely useful for small teams
  • Marketing automation built-in, no extra purchase needed
  • Easy onboarding – most users are productive within a day
  • Clean, modern UI that doesn’t require training
  • Strong customer support (chat, phone, email) even on free plans

HubSpot Cons

  • Sales and Marketing hubs are sold separately – can get expensive if you need both at scale
  • Limited customization compared to Salesforce
  • Reporting can feel restrictive without Professional plan ($100/user/month)
  • Contact storage limit on free plan (1M contacts okay, but activity data is limited unless upgraded)

Salesforce Pros

  • Unmatched customization – you can build exactly what you want
  • Massive app ecosystem (AppExchange)
  • Powerful reporting and analytics
  • Scales to enterprise without needing to switch platforms

Salesforce Cons

  • Steep learning curve – expect to hire a consultant or spend weeks learning
  • Cost adds up fast – $25 base + email tracking add-on + marketing add-on + likely a consultant fee
  • Mobile app is clunky
  • Support can be slow on lower-tier plans
  • Out-of-the-box experience is mediocre – you have to customize everything to Make it usable

Final Verdict: Which CRM Should a Small Business Choose?

If you’re a small business – meaning fewer than 50 employees, limited budget, and no dedicated Salesforce admin – choose HubSpot.

It’s not even close. HubSpot’s free and starter plans give you a functioning CRM with email tracking, pipeline management, and meeting scheduling in hours. Sales forces you to pay $25/user/month for a system that still lacks email tracking out of the box. The onboarding speed, built-in marketing tools, and overall user experience make HubSpot the better fit for 9 out of 10 small businesses.

That said, Salesforce is still the right choice if:

  • Your team has someone who can (or wants to) become a Salesforce admin
  • You need extreme customization (custom objects, complex approval workflows)
  • You’re planning to grow to 200+ employees and want a single platform to scale
  • You already use other Salesforce products (Service Cloud, Marketing Cloud) and want everything under one roof

But for the typical freelancer, startup, or small agency? HubSpot. Every time.

FAQ: HubSpot vs Salesforce for Small Business

Q: Can I use Salesforce for free?

A: No. Salesforce offers a 30-day trial, but there’s no permanent free tier. HubSpot has a generous free plan that works for most small businesses.

Q: Which CRM is easier to learn?

A: HubSpot, by a wide margin. You can be productive within a few hours. Salesforce expects you to understand objects, fields, and profiles before you’re comfortable.

Q: Is HubSpot good for B2B sales?

A: Yes. It handles deal stages, pipeline management, and email sequences well. The free plan even supports multiple pipelines and deal rotation.

Q: Does Salesforce have better reporting?

A: Yes, but only if you have the time and skill to use it. HubSpot’s reporting is simpler but more limited, especially on lower-tier plans.

Q: Which CRM is better for startups on a tight budget?

A: HubSpot. The free plan covers the basics. Salesforce’s minimum cost is $25/user/month with very limited features – you’ll end up paying more to get what HubSpot gives you for free.

Q: Can I migrate from HubSpot to Salesforce later?

A: Yes, but it’s painful. Export your contacts and deals from HubSpot, then import into Salesforce. You’ll lose some activity history and automation rules. It’s better to pick the right CRM from the start.

Q: Do I need to pay for both Sales and Marketing Hub?

A: Not necessarily. You can use Sales Hub for free and add only Marketing Hub Starter ($20/user/month) when you need email marketing. Salesforce requires separate purchases for any marketing functionality.

Still torn? Start with HubSpot’s free plan. If you outgrow it (which might take years), you can always consider migrating. But for most small businesses, HubSpot is all the CRM you’ll ever need.
