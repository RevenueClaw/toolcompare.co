Let’s be honest: picking a live chat tool for your SaaS is a bit like choosing a co-founder. You’re going to spend every day with it, it’s going to talk to your customers, and if you pick wrong, you’ll feel it in your churn rate.

I’ve spent the last two weeks running Intercom, Drift, and Crisp through real-world scenarios—not just reading marketing pages. I set up test accounts, threw fake customer queries at them, measured response times, and looked at what happens when you actually need to scale.

Here’s the short version: all three are solid, but they solve different problems. The one that’s right for you depends on whether you’re a bootstrapped startup, a growth-stage team, or an enterprise with a dedicated support org.

Let’s break it down.

The Quick Overview

Before we get into the weeds, here’s where each tool sits:

  • Intercom is the full-suite heavyweight. It does live chat, but it’s really a customer communication platform with email, in-app messages, and a help center.
  • Drift (now part of Salesloft) is laser-focused on conversational marketing. It’s built to turn website visitors into qualified leads and book meetings.
  • Crisp is the budget-friendly all-rounder. It gives you chat, email, and a shared inbox without the enterprise price tag.

Right off the bat, you can see the problem: people compare all three on “live chat” when they’re actually buying different things. A quick note—if you’re looking for a tool that’s purely for support tickets, none of these are your best bet. You’d want something like Zendesk. But if you want chat that does more, keep reading.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

Intercom: The Swiss Army Knife (That Costs Like One)

Intercom is the tool everyone names when they say “we need live chat.” It’s been around forever, it’s polished, and it does a lot.

Chat & Messaging

The chat widget itself is smooth. It’s customizable, supports rich media, and you can send targeted messages based on user behavior. For example, if a user has been on your pricing page for 30 seconds, you can trigger a message saying “Need help choosing a plan?”

That’s powerful. But here’s the catch: the chat is just the tip of the iceberg. Intercom’s real strength is the journey—you can build automated sequences that combine emails, in-app messages, and push notifications. It’s basically a mini marketing automation tool.

Help Center & Bots

The built-in help center (Articles) is decent, and the answer bot (Fin) can resolve basic queries. But setting up Fin properly takes work. You need to feed it your help docs, train it on intents, and test it. If you’re a small team, that’s a project, not a quick fix.

The Downside

Intercom’s pricing is aggressive. The Essential plan starts around $74/month per seat, but that’s just the base. You’ll pay extra for the answer bot, extra for multiple inboxes, extra for reporting. By the time you add what you actually need, you’re looking at $200–$400 per month per seat. For a 5-person team, that’s a serious dent in your Runway.

Drift: Built for Sales, Not Support

Drift is the odd one out here. It’s not really a support tool—it’s a revenue tool. The entire platform is designed around one question: “Can we turn this visitor into a meeting?”

Conversational Marketing

Drift’s chat is aggressive (in a good way, if you’re selling). It uses playbooks to qualify leads, route them to the right rep, and book meetings directly on your calendar. If you’re a B2B SaaS with a sales team, this is gold. It cuts down the time between “visitor lands” and “demo booked” from days to minutes.

The Bots

Drift’s bots are more advanced than Intercom’s in one specific way: they’re built for lead qualification. You can set up a bot that asks “What’s your company size?” and “What’s your budget?” and then routes the conversation based on the answers. That’s genuinely useful.

The Downside

For support, Drift is weak. There’s no shared inbox for email, no robust ticketing system, and the reporting is sales-focused. If you try to use Drift as your primary support channel, you’ll end up frustrated. Also, the pricing is similar to Intercom—the Advanced plan is around $100/month per seat, and you’ll need the Pro plan ($500+/month) to get the really good stuff.

Crisp: The Underdog That Does the Job

Crisp is the tool that doesn’t get the hype, but it’s quietly the most practical for small SaaS teams. It’s a French company, and it shows—the design is clean, the pricing is sane, and the feature set is surprisingly complete.

Chat & Shared Inbox

Crisp’s chat is solid. It’s fast, it’s customizable, and it handles the basics well. The real gem is the shared inbox—you get chat, email, and social messages (Twitter, Facebook, Instagram) all in one place. That’s a huge time-saver if you’re a small team juggling multiple channels.

Bots & Automation

Crisp has a bot builder that’s visual and easy to use. You can set up simple rules (“If a user asks about pricing, show them the pricing page”) without writing code. It’s not as advanced as Drift’s playbooks, but for 90% of support queries, it’s more than enough.

The Downside

Crisp’s reporting is basic. You can see volume, response times, and customer satisfaction, but you won’t get the deep behavioral analytics that Intercom offers. Also, the app marketplace is smaller. If you need a deep integration with Salesforce or HubSpot, you might hit a wall.

Pros and Cons

Intercom Pros

  • All-in-one: Chat, email, help center, and in-app messaging in one platform.
  • Powerful automation: The journeys feature is genuinely impressive for onboarding and retention.
  • Polished UX: Your support team will like using it. That matters more than you think.

Intercom Cons

  • Expensive: The price scales quickly, and you pay extra for every useful add-on.
  • Overkill for small teams: If you just need a chat widget, this is like buying a Ferrari to drive to the grocery store.
  • Setup complexity: Getting the bots and journeys right takes time and expertise.

Drift Pros

  • Sales-focused: If your goal is booking demos, nothing beats Drift.
  • Advanced playbooks: The lead qualification bots are smart and save your sales team time.
  • Meeting scheduler: The built-in calendar integration streamlines the demo process.

Drift Cons

  • Weak for support: No email inbox, no ticketing, and the reporting isn’t built for CS teams.
  • Pricey: You’ll pay a premium for the sales features, even if you only use chat.
  • Aggressive by default: If you’re not running an outbound sales motion, the bots can feel pushy to visitors.

Crisp Pros

  • Affordable: The Pro plan is $25/seat, which is a fraction of Intercom or Drift.
  • Easy setup: You can have it running in 15 minutes.
  • Unified inbox: Email, chat, and social all in one place—perfect for small teams.

Crisp Cons

  • Limited reporting: You won’t get deep insights into user behavior.
  • Basic automation: The bot builder is fine for simple rules, but it’s not a marketing automation tool.
  • Smaller ecosystem: Fewer integrations and less community support.

Real-World Test: Which One Actually Saves You Money?

I ran a simple scenario: a SaaS company with 5 support agents, handling 1,000 conversations a month.

With Intercom: You’re paying $74/seat for Essential, but you’ll quickly need the Advanced plan (around $110/seat) to get the good reporting. Plus, you’ll add the Fin bot at $0.99 per resolution (which adds up fast). Realistic cost: $600–$700/month.

With Drift: The Advanced plan is $100/seat, but you’ll need the Pro plan ($500+/month) for the full playbook features. Realistic cost: $1,000+/month (and that’s before you realize you need a separate email tool).

With Crisp: The Pro plan is $25/seat. You get chat, email, and basic bots. Realistic cost: $125/month.

Here’s the kicker: for 90% of support conversations, Crisp handled them just as well as Intercom. The response times were similar, the widget was just as fast, and the shared inbox was actually easier to manage.

The other 10%—the complex automation, the deep behavioral tracking, the sales playbooks—that’s where you’d need Intercom or Drift. But here’s the question: does your SaaS actually need that 10%? If you’re selling a $10/month product, probably not. If you’re selling a $10,000/year enterprise plan, maybe.

The Verdict

Here’s where I land, and I know this will annoy some people.

If you’re a small SaaS (under 20 employees) with a support-focused team: choose Crisp. It’s not the flashiest, but it does the job, it’s affordable, and the unified inbox saves you from juggling four different tools. Check Crisp ->

If you’re a B2B SaaS with a sales team and you’re trying to book demos: choose Drift. It’s the only one of the three that’s actually built for revenue conversations. Just don’t try to use it for support. Check Drift ->

If you’re an enterprise with complex needs, a dedicated support org, and a budget: choose Intercom. It’s the most complete platform, and the automation capabilities are unmatched. Just be prepared for the cost. Check Intercom ->

My personal pick for most SaaS businesses? Crisp. It’s the rare tool that actually respects your budget while doing 80% of what the expensive guys do. And in this economy, that 20% you’re missing is often not worth the extra $500/month.

FAQ

Q: Can I switch from Intercom to Crisp easily?

A: Yes, but it takes a few days. You’ll need to export your chat history (Crisp supports CSV import), recreate your help center articles, and rebuild any automations. Start with a trial run for a week before you fully commit.

Q: Does Drift have a free plan?

A: No, but they offer a 14-day free trial. The trial includes most features, so you can test the playbooks before paying.

Q: Which tool has the best mobile app?

A: Crisp’s mobile app is the most responsive for support agents. Intercom’s app is good but feels bloated. Drift’s app is fine for sales but not great for managing support tickets.

Q: Is Crisp good for product-led growth companies?

A: Yes, actually. Crisp has in-app messaging and mobile push notifications, which are essential for onboarding and feature announcements. It’s not as advanced as Intercom’s journeys, but it’s more than enough for most PLG teams.

Q: What about GDPR compliance?

A: All three are GDPR compliant. Crisp is EU-based (France), which is a plus if you’re serving European customers and want to avoid US data transfer issues.

Q: Can I use these tools for email marketing?

A: Intercom and Crisp both support email campaigns. Drift does not—it’s purely conversational. If email is a core part of your strategy, stick with Intercom or Crisp.
