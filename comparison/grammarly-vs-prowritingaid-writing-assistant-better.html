Grammarly vs ProWritingAid: Which Writing Assistant is Better in 2026?

If you write for a living—emails, blog posts, reports, or even that novel gathering dust on your desktop—you’ve probably tried at least one AI writing assistant. Grammarly and ProWritingAid are the two heavyweights, but they serve different crowds. After spending weeks testing both side-by-side, I’ll break down exactly where each shines, where they fall short, and which one actually saves you money.

Let’s get one thing straight: neither tool will magically turn you into Hemingway. But used correctly, they can catch embarrassing typos, tighten your prose, and help you develop better writing habits. The real question is which one fits your workflow and budget.

What They Both Do Well

Both Grammarly and ProWritingAid check your grammar, spelling, punctuation, and style. They integrate with browsers, Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and other platforms. Both offer desktop apps and browser extensions. Both aim to improve your writing in real-time.

But that’s where the similarity ends. Their approaches, depth of analysis, and pricing structures are very different.

Feature Breakdown: Grammarly

Grammarly is the more polished, consumer-friendly option. It’s designed to be unobtrusive—you write, it underlines issues, you click to fix. The free version catches basic errors, but the Premium tier adds full-sentence rewrites, tone detection, and plagiarism checking.

Key features of Grammarly Premium:

  • Tone detector: Helps you sound confident, friendly, or formal depending on context.
  • Clarity suggestions: Reorganizes awkward sentences and suggests more direct wording.
  • Full-sentence rewrites: For a fee, Grammarly will rewrite entire sentences using AI.
  • Plagiarism checker: Scans billions of web pages (useful for students, but limited for long-form content).
  • Genre-specific goals: You can set a goal for domain (e.g., academic, business, casual), audience, and formality.
  • Integrations: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, Microsoft Office, Google Docs, and a desktop app for Windows and Mac.

What Grammarly doesn’t do well:

  • Advanced style reports. Grammarly gives you a weekly email summary of your writing stats, but it’s light compared to ProWritingAid’s deep-dive reports.
  • Customization. You can’t tweak the rules much—it’s a one-size-fits-all approach.
  • Offline use. The desktop app requires internet for most features.

Feature Breakdown: ProWritingAid

ProWritingAid is built for writers who want to understand why their writing needs fixing. It’s more of a writing coach than a quick fixer. The free version is extremely generous, and the Premium plan unlocks 20+ reports that analyze everything from sentence length to clichés to overused words.

Key features of ProWritingAid Premium:

  • 20+ writing reports: Includes readability, sentence variation, sticky sentences (overused words like “very,” “really”), dialogue tags, and more.
  • Style consistency: Checks for repeated words, sentence starts, and pacing.
  • In-depth grammar checks: More sensitive to nuanced issues like passive voice misuse and wordy phrases.
  • Thesaurus integration: Right-click any word to see synonyms with context.
  • Integrations: Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Scrivener, OpenOffice, and a desktop app.
  • Lifetime license option: One-time payment for lifetime access (rare in the SaaS world).
  • Plagiarism checker: Available as a paid add-on (separate from Premium).

What ProWritingAid doesn’t do well:

  • Real-time tone detection. It’s less intuitive for casual emails or social media posts.
  • UI can feel overwhelming. The reports are powerful but take time to learn.
  • Fewer “rewrite” suggestions. ProWritingAid flags issues but doesn’t offer as many one-click rewrites as Grammarly.

Pricing: What You Actually Pay

Grammarly offers a free tier that catches basic errors. Premium is $12/month billed annually ($144/year), or $30/month if you pay monthly. There’s also a Business plan at $25/user/month.

ProWritingAid’s free tier is surprisingly generous: you get access to several reports and a daily word limit. Premium is $10/month billed annually ($120/year), or $20/month monthly. The lifetime option is $399 one-time—great if you plan to use it for years.

Verdict on pricing: ProWritingAid wins for budget-conscious writers. The lifetime license pays for itself in under 4 years. Grammarly’s Premium is pricier but includes plagiarism checking and more polished real-time feedback.

Pros and Cons

Grammarly

Pros:

  • Excellent real-time suggestions—fast and easy to apply.
  • Tone detector helps avoid sounding rude or overly casual.
  • Plagiarism checker included (handy for students).
  • Clean, minimal interface that doesn’t distract.
  • Works well for short-form writing (emails, social media, comments).

Cons:

  • Expensive annual subscription compared to alternatives.
  • Limited customisation—you can’t adjust rules.
  • No offline mode.
  • Advanced style analysis is weak.

ProWritingAid

Pros:

  • Unmatched depth of analysis—reports on pacing, clichés, overused words, sentence length variation.
  • Lifetime license option saves money long-term.
  • Works offline (desktop app).
  • Integrates with Scrivener and more niche writing tools.
  • Free version is very usable.

Cons:

  • No real-time tone detection.
  • UI can be cluttered and intimidating for new users.
  • Plagiarism checker costs extra.
  • Fewer one-click rewrites—more manual editing required.

Who Should Use Which?

Choose Grammarly if:

  • You write mostly emails, reports, and short business documents.
  • You want a “set it and forget it” assistant that catches errors without much effort.
  • You need plagiarism checking included.
  • You value tone feedback for professional communication.

Choose ProWritingAid if:

  • You write long-form content (blog posts, articles, novels, academic papers).
  • You want to improve your writing skills over time, not just fix mistakes.
  • You’re on a budget and prefer a one-time payment.
  • You need deeper analysis of style and readability.

Final Verdict

There’s no universal winner—it depends on your writing habits and budget.

For most business users and casual writers: Grammarly is the better choice. Its tone detection and one-click fixes save time and prevent embarrassing mistakes. The price is higher, but the polished experience justifies it if you write daily.

For serious writers, editors, and students: ProWritingAid offers far more value. The lifetime license is a steal if you plan to use it for years, and the 20+ reports help you become a better writer—not just a cleaner one.

If you can afford both, consider using Grammarly for real-time checks and ProWritingAid for final editing passes. But if I had to pick one for my own work (I write long-form articles and some emails), I’d go with ProWritingAid Premium annual—it’s cheaper, more insightful, and the offline mode comes in handy when traveling.

FAQ

Q: Can I use both Grammarly and ProWritingAid at the same time?

A: Yes, but expect some overlap. They both underline suggestions, and having both active in the same doc can be distracting. I recommend using Grammarly as you write and ProWritingAid for a final review.

Q: Which has better plagiarism detection?

A: Grammarly’s is included in Premium and works well for web-based sources. ProWritingAid’s is a paid add-on and is more geared toward academic papers.

Q: Do either work offline?

A: Grammarly’s desktop app requires an internet connection for most features. ProWritingAid’s desktop app works fully offline once installed.

Q: Which is better for fiction writers?

A: ProWritingAid. Its reports on dialogue tags, overused words, and sentence length variation are invaluable for fiction editing. Grammarly is too generic for novelists.

Q: Can I cancel anytime?

A: Both offer monthly subscriptions that you can cancel anytime. Annual plans are non-refundable after the first 30 days typically.

Q: Do they support other languages?

A: Grammarly supports English only (with multiple dialects: US, UK, Canada, Australia). ProWritingAid also supports English only, but with the same dialect options.

Q: Which one is cheaper?

A: ProWritingAid’s annual plan is $120 vs Grammarly’s $144. And the lifetime option makes ProWritingAid much cheaper over 3+ years.
