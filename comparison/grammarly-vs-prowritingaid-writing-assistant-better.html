Grammarly vs ProWritingAid: Which Writing Assistant Is Better in 2026?

Most people lump Grammarly and ProWritingAid into the same bucket: "grammar checker." That's a bit like calling a Swiss Army knife and a chef's knife the same thing. They both cut, but you'd only want one of them for filleting a fish.

Grammarly is a real-time correctness layer that follows you around the internet — your inbox, your Slack messages, your Google Docs, your LinkedIn posts. ProWritingAid is an editing workshop you sit down inside, with more than 20 diagnostic reports that tell you why your paragraph feels sluggish, not just that you typed "their" instead of "there."

That difference drives everything else: the pricing, the learning curve, and which one is actually worth your money. Below is a full breakdown based on published specs, current pricing pages, and aggregated user reports from Trustpilot, Reddit, and G2.

The 30-second answer

  • Pick Grammarly if you write short-to-medium pieces all day — emails, proposals, client updates, blog drafts — and you want corrections to appear as you type, everywhere.
  • Pick ProWritingAid if you write long-form (books, novels, 3,000-word articles, academic papers) and you want deep style analysis plus the option to pay once and never subscribe again.

Everything else is nuance. Let's get into it.

What each tool actually is

Grammarly

Launched in 2009 by three Ukrainian developers, Grammarly built its reputation on one thing: catching mistakes in real time without slowing you down. The company's parent organization rebranded to Superhuman in late 2025, but the product you install is still called Grammarly, and the browser extension behaves the same way it always has.

Grammarly organizes its suggestions into four buckets: Correctness (grammar, spelling, punctuation), Clarity (wordiness, passive voice, confusing phrasing), Engagement (word variety, sentence variety), and Delivery (tone, confidence, formality). It also reads the tone of what you've written and tells you how it's likely to land — a feature that sounds gimmicky until you send an email that reads angry when you meant it to read firm.

The free tier is unusually generous. You get the core grammar, spelling, and punctuation engine permanently, plus a small monthly allowance of AI prompts.

Check Grammarly

ProWritingAid

ProWritingAid came out of the UK in 2012, built by a team who cared less about real-time correction and more about the editing process. Where Grammarly flags a sentence, ProWritingAid writes you a report card on it.
