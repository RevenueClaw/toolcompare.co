Grammarly vs ProWritingAid: Which Writing Assistant is Better in 2026?

You’re staring at a blank document, deadline looming. You know your writing could use a polish, but you don’t want to spend hours proofreading. Enter Grammarly and ProWritingAid — the two heavyweights of writing assistants. Both promise to catch grammar mistakes, improve style, and Make you sound smarter. But which one actually delivers without burning a hole in your wallet?

I’ve used both extensively over the past few months, testing them on emails, blog posts, academic papers, and even a few Slack messages. Here’s my honest take on Grammarly vs ProWritingAid in 2026, broken down by the features that matter most.

What They Do (and Don’t)

Before we get into the nitty-gritty, let’s set the stage. Both tools are AI-powered writing assistants that check grammar, spelling, punctuation, and style. They integrate with browsers, Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and other apps. But they have different strengths.

Grammarly is the mainstream favorite — it’s fast, clean, and works everywhere. ProWritingAid is the deep-dive editor, offering detailed reports on everything from sentence length to overused words. Think of Grammarly as the friendly copy editor who catches your typos, and ProWritingAid as the writing coach who gives you a full performance review.

Feature Breakdown

Grammar & Spelling Accuracy

Both tools catch the obvious stuff: missing commas, typos, subject-verb agreement. But I tested them on some tricky sentences — like “The committee were divided” (should be “was” in American English) and “I could care less” (should be “couldn’t care less”).

Winner: Grammarly — It caught nearly all of them, including context-specific errors. ProWritingAid missed a few subtle ones, especially with British vs American English settings. Grammarly’s contextual understanding is slightly better, partly because it has more user data to train on.

Style & Tone Suggestions

Grammarly’s tone detector is a standout. It tells you if your writing sounds confident, friendly, or formal — and suggests tweaks. ProWritingAid doesn’t have a tone detector, but its style suggestions are more granular. It flags passive voice, adverbs, clichés, and even wordy phrases like “in order to” (just use “to”).

If you’re writing marketing copy or emails, Grammarly’s tone hints are handy. If you’re writing a novel or a long report, ProWritingAid’s style reports are more valuable.

Winner: Tie — depends on your use case.

Integrations & Workflow

Grammarly wins on breadth. It has a desktop app, browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge), mobile Keyboard, Microsoft Office add-in, and Google Docs support. It even works in Slack, Gmail, and Notion. ProWritingAid covers the basics: browser extension, Word, Google Docs, and a desktop app for Windows/Mac. But it lacks a mobile keyboard and doesn’t integrate with as many web apps.

Winner: Grammarly — if you live in your browser or on your phone, Grammarly is more seamless.

Plagiarism Checker

Both offer plagiarism detection, but only in their premium plans. Grammarly’s checker compares your text against 16 billion web pages and academic databases. ProWritingAid checks against the web and a smaller database. In my tests, Grammarly caught more matches, including a snippet from a blog post I’d accidentally paraphrased too closely.

Winner: Grammarly — more thorough, though ProWritingAid’s is good enough for most casual checks.

AI Writing Features (2026 Updates)

Both tools now include generative AI features — think “rewrite this paragraph” or “expand on this idea.” Grammarly’s AI (powered by its own models) lets you generate text from a prompt, adjust tone, and shorten or lengthen content. It’s surprisingly good for drafting emails or social posts.

ProWritingAid’s AI, called “ProWritingAid Coach,” is more limited. It can rewrite sentences for clarity and suggest alternative phrasing, but it doesn’t generate original content. It’s more of a rephrasing assistant than a writing partner.

Winner: Grammarly — better for writers who want to generate ideas quickly.

Reports & Analytics

This is where ProWritingAid shines. It gives you 20+ detailed reports: readability, sentence length variation, pacing, sticky sentences (ones that are hard to read), alliteration, and even a “diction” report that highlights informal language. You can see a summary of your writing habits over time.

Grammarly’s analytics are minimal — a weekly email with your “performance stats” (like words written, errors corrected, tone used). Useful, but not deep.

Winner: ProWritingAid — if you’re serious about improving your writing, these reports are gold.

Pros and Cons

Grammarly Pros

  • Excellent grammar and spelling accuracy
  • Tone detection helps polish emails and social posts
  • Wide integration (browser, mobile, desktop)
  • AI writing features for generating content
  • Clean, user-friendly interface
  • Plagiarism checker is thorough

Grammarly Cons

  • Expensive monthly (no lifetime option)
  • Overly aggressive suggestions sometimes (e.g., changing “I think” to “I believe” for no reason)
  • Can be distracting in Google Docs with lots of underlines
  • Reports are shallow — no deep style analysis

ProWritingAid Pros

  • Deep style reports (readability, pacing, overused words)
  • Lifetime plan is a steal for long-term users
  • More affordable monthly/annual plans
  • Excellent for fiction writers and academics
  • Customizable settings (e.g., genre-specific style guides)

ProWritingAid Cons

  • Less accurate on subtle grammar errors
  • No tone detection or AI generation
  • Fewer integrations (no mobile keyboard, limited web app support)
  • Interface feels cluttered with all those reports
  • Plagiarism checker is weaker and costs extra

Who Should Use Which?

Choose Grammarly if:

  • You write a lot of emails, social media, or business communication
  • You want a tool that works everywhere (including your phone)
  • You need AI help drafting content quickly
  • You’re okay paying a monthly premium for convenience

Choose ProWritingAid if:

  • You write long-form content (books, reports, academic papers)
  • You want to improve your writing style over time with detailed feedback
  • You’re on a tight budget and want a lifetime license
  • You don’t need tone detection or AI generation

Final Verdict

After testing both side by side for a month, I’d say Grammarly wins for most people — especially professionals, marketers, and casual writers. It’s faster, more accurate, and integrates better into your daily workflow. The AI writing features in 2026 are a nice bonus.

But if you’re a novelist, a student writing a thesis, or someone who wants to deeply improve your writing craft, ProWritingAid is the better choice. Its reports are unmatched, and the lifetime plan makes it the more economical option in the long run.

My pick: For 90% of users, Grammarly Premium is worth the extra cost. For the other 10% (especially writers who need deep analysis), ProWritingAid is a no-brainer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use both Grammarly and ProWritingAid together?

Yes, but it can get messy. Both will underline the same text with different suggestions. I’d recommend using one as your primary checker and the other only for specific reports (e.g., run a ProWritingAid report on a finished draft).

Which tool is better for academic writing?

ProWritingAid. Its reports on readability, sentence length, and overused words are perfect for tightening up essays and theses. Plus, it supports APA, MLA, and Chicago style guides.

Does Grammarly work offline?

Grammarly’s desktop app works offline, but the browser extension needs an internet connection. ProWritingAid’s desktop app also works offline.

Is the free plan enough?

Grammarly’s free plan is excellent for basic grammar and spelling. ProWritingAid’s free plan is more limited — you only get 10 suggestions per report. If you write a lot, you’ll hit that cap quickly.

Which tool is better for non-native English speakers?

Grammarly. Its tone detection and clarity suggestions are more helpful for learners. ProWritingAid’s reports can be overwhelming if you’re still building vocabulary.

Can I get a refund if I don’t like it?

Grammarly offers a 30-day money-back guarantee on annual plans. ProWritingAid has a 14-day refund policy on monthly and annual plans, but no refunds on lifetime purchases.

Still unsure? Both tools offer free trials — try Grammarly Premium for 7 days or ProWritingAid Premium for 14 days. See which one fits your writing style.
