Grammarly vs ProWritingAid: Which Writing Assistant is Better in 2026?

You’ve probably heard the names. Grammarly is the household name in grammar checking. ProWritingAid is the underdog that writing nerds swear by. But which one actually deserves a spot in your workflow — and your budget?

I’ve used both extensively over the past year, testing them on blog posts, emails, academic writing, and even fiction. I’ve compared their accuracy, integrations, pricing, and the quality of their suggestions. Here’s everything you need to know to Make the right call.

Quick Verdict

Choose Grammarly if: You want a polished, real-time assistant that works everywhere you write (browser, desktop, mobile). It’s better for quick fixes, professional emails, and non-writers who need a safety net.

Choose ProWritingAid if: You’re a serious writer — novelist, blogger, or student — who wants deep stylistic analysis, detailed reports, and a tool that helps you grow as a writer over time. It’s more affordable and offers features Grammarly simply doesn’t.

Overall winner for most people: Grammarly edges ahead for general use, but ProWritingAid wins for writers who care about craft.

What Is Grammarly?

Grammarly is the most popular AI writing assistant on the planet. It started as a simple grammar checker and has evolved into a full-blown writing platform that checks spelling, grammar, punctuation, clarity, engagement, and tone.

It works as a browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari), a desktop app (Windows and Mac), a mobile keyboard (iOS and Android), and an add-in for Microsoft Office. It also has a web editor where you can upload documents.

Grammarly’s core strength is its ease of use. It runs in the background, highlighting issues in real time. You click to accept suggestions. It’s like having an editor looking over your shoulder, but one that never gets annoyed.

In 2025, Grammarly added more generative AI features (like writing prompts and rewriting suggestions) through GrammarlyGO. The premium tier now includes full-sentence rewrites, tone detection, and plagiarism checking.

Pricing: Free (basic corrections), Premium ($12/month billed annually), Business ($15/user/month billed annually).

What Is ProWritingAid?

ProWritingAid is a writing assistant aimed at serious writers. It started as a tool for novelists and has expanded to cover all types of writing, but its DNA is still in deep analysis.

Instead of just flagging errors, ProWritingAid offers 20+ detailed reports: readability, overused words, sentence length variation, clichés, sticky sentences, pacing, and more. It’s like having a writing coach who gives you a full diagnostic after every draft.

It integrates with Word, Google Docs, Scrivener, Final Draft, and most browsers. It also has a desktop app (Windows and Mac) and a web editor. There’s no mobile keyboard, which is a notable gap.

ProWritingAid’s strength is in helping you improve your writing over time. The reports show you patterns — not just one-off mistakes. If you’re a novelist or a content writer who cares about style, this is invaluable.

Pricing: Free (limited checks, 500 words per check), Premium ($10/month billed annually), Premium Pro ($12/month billed annually – adds plagiarism checker). There’s also a lifetime option ($399 one-time).

Feature Comparison

Grammar and Spelling Accuracy

Both tools catch most common errors. In my testing on a 1,000-word blog post, Grammarly caught 95% of mistakes (including missing commas, subject-verb agreement, and typos). ProWritingAid caught about 90% — it missed a few subtle comma splices.

Grammarly’s real-time detection is faster and less intrusive. ProWritingAid sometimes requires you to run a report to see all errors, though its inline checker works well in Word and Google Docs.

Winner: Grammarly, by a small margin.

Style and Tone Suggestions

Grammarly’s tone detector is excellent. It tells you if your email sounds friendly, confident, or formal. It also suggests synonyms and rephrases for clarity.

ProWritingAid goes deeper. Its style report flags passive voice, adverbs, vague words, and sentence starts. It’s not as polished in real-time, but the depth is unmatched.

Winner: ProWritingAid for serious writers; Grammarly for quick tone adjustments.

Plagiarism Checker

Both have plagiarism checkers in their premium plans. Grammarly’s checks against 16 billion web pages. ProWritingAid’s checks against 1 billion (and requires the more expensive Premium Pro plan). In my tests, Grammarly was more accurate and faster.

Winner: Grammarly.

Integrations

Grammarly works everywhere: browsers, Office, Google Docs, and mobile. ProWritingAid covers browsers, Office, Google Docs, plus Scrivener and Final Draft — a big win for novelists and screenwriters. But ProWritingAid lacks a mobile keyboard.

Winner: Tie — depends on your needs.

Writing Reports (ProWritingAid’s Killer Feature)

ProWritingAid offers 20+ reports: Readability, Overused Words, Sentence Length, Sticky Sentences, Diction, Pacing, and more. Grammarly has nothing comparable. If you’re writing a novel or a long-form article, these reports are gold.

Winner: ProWritingAid, hands down.

Pros and Cons of Grammarly

Pros:

  • Excellent real-time grammar and spelling correction
  • Tone detector is intuitive and useful
  • Works on mobile (keyboard app)
  • Plagiarism checker is robust
  • Easy to use, minimal learning curve

Cons:

  • Expensive for annual premium ($144/yr)
  • No lifetime plan
  • No deep writing reports (no pacing, overused words, etc.)
  • Can be too aggressive with suggestions (sometimes changes your voice)
  • Privacy concerns (some users dislike data handling)

Pros and Cons of ProWritingAid

Pros:

  • 20+ detailed writing reports (unmatched depth)
  • Cheaper annual plan ($120/yr)
  • Lifetime option available ($399)
  • Works with Scrivener and Final Draft
  • Focus on improving writing skills, not just fixing errors

Cons:

  • No mobile keyboard
  • Real-time grammar checking is less accurate than Grammarly
  • Steeper learning curve (reports can be overwhelming)
  • Plagiarism checker only in higher-priced plan
  • Browser extension can be slow on some sites

Who Should Choose Grammarly?

  • Busy professionals who need quick, reliable proofreading in emails, Slack, and documents.
  • Non-native English speakers who want a safety net for grammar and tone.
  • Anyone who writes on mobile (Grammarly’s keyboard is excellent).
  • Teams that need consistent brand tone and style guides (Grammarly Business).
  • Casual bloggers who want clean posts without deep analysis.

Who Should Choose ProWritingAid?

  • Novelists and fiction writers who need pacing reports, dialogue tags analysis, and overused word checks.
  • Academic writers who want to avoid passive voice and improve sentence variety.
  • Content marketers who produce long-form articles and want to optimize readability.
  • Budget-conscious writers who want a lifetime license.
  • Anyone who wants to become a better writer (ProWritingAid’s reports teach you).

Final Verdict

Grammarly is the better choice for 80% of users. It’s more polished, works everywhere, and catches more errors in real time. If you write emails, social media, and occasional blog posts, Grammarly will serve you well.

ProWritingAid is the better choice for serious writers. If you’re writing a novel, a thesis, or regular long-form content, the depth of ProWritingAid’s analysis is worth the trade-off in real-time accuracy. And the lifetime plan makes it a smarter long-term investment.

My pick: I use both. Grammarly for quick checks and email. ProWritingAid for long drafts and final reports. But if I had to choose one, I’d pick ProWritingAid because it helps me grow as a writer. Your mileage may vary.

FAQ

Can I use Grammarly and ProWritingAid together?

Yes, they can run side by side. I do it. Just be prepared for double underlines. Disable one when it gets annoying.

Which is better for academic writing?

ProWritingAid, because of its reports on passive voice, sentence length, and readability. Grammarly is fine for basic proofreading but lacks the analytical depth.

Does ProWritingAid have a free plan?

Yes, but it’s limited to 500 words per check. Grammarly’s free plan is more generous (full document length, but fewer suggestions).

Is the lifetime plan for ProWritingAid worth it?

If you plan to use it for more than 3-4 years, yes. It’s $399 one-time vs $120/year. Break-even is about 3.3 years.

Which tool is better for fiction writers?

ProWritingAid, by a mile. Its pacing, dialogue, and overused words reports are designed for fiction.

Does Grammarly work with Google Docs?

Yes, both work with Google Docs. Grammarly’s integration is smoother and faster.

Which has better customer support?

Both have decent support via email and knowledge bases. Grammarly has live chat for Business users.

Can I get a refund if I don’t like it?

Both offer 30-day money-back guarantees on annual plans.

Is there a student discount?

ProWritingAid offers 20% off for students (verified via Student Beans). Grammarly does not have a student discount.

Which one is more affordable?

ProWritingAid, especially with the lifetime plan. Grammarly’s annual is $144 vs ProWritingAid’s $120.
