Hubspot vs Salesforce for Small Business: Which CRM is Right for You?

If you run a small business and you’re shopping for a CRM, you’ve probably heard two names over and over: HubSpot and Salesforce. They’re the heavyweights, but they approach the market from very different angles. HubSpot sells itself as the friendly, all-in-one platform that grows with you. Salesforce is the enterprise giant that can handle almost anything — if you’re willing to pay for it and spend time setting it up.

I’ve used both tools extensively. I’ve built workflows in Salesforce that felt like programming a spaceship. I’ve also used HubSpot for a 10-person team where we just wanted something that worked out of the box. The differences are real, and picking the wrong one can cost you thousands in wasted time and licensing fees.

This comparison will break down exactly where each CRM shines, where it falls short, and which one actually makes sense for a small business. No fluff, no marketing speak — just what you need to know.

HubSpot vs Salesforce: The 30,000-Foot View

Let’s start with the core philosophy difference. HubSpot is built around the idea of “start free, pay later.” Their free CRM is genuinely useful, and they Make money by upselling you on marketing, sales, and service hubs as you grow. Salesforce, on the other hand, is built for customization and scale from day one — but it comes with a steeper learning curve and a price tag that can balloon fast.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

Ease of Use

HubSpot wins this hands-down. The interface is clean, modern, and forgiving. You can start logging contacts and deals in minutes without any training. The pipeline view is drag-and-drop, and the sidebar on each contact record shows everything: emails, notes, tasks, meeting history. It’s designed to reduce clicks.

Salesforce’s classic interface (still used by many) feels like a spreadsheet from 2005. The newer Lightning Experience is better, but it’s still dense. There are tabs, subtabs, search bars, and dropdown menus everywhere. Setting up a simple pipeline takes several steps: creating the object, fields, page layouts, and then the actual pipeline stages. It’s powerful, but you’ll need to watch a few YouTube tutorials (or hire a consultant) to get it right.

Verdict: HubSpot for ease of use. Salesforce if you have time to learn.

Contact & Deal Management

Both tools let you store contacts, companies, deals, and activities. HubSpot gives you a clean timeline view per contact, automatic logging of emails (if you connect your inbox), and built-in meeting scheduling (with paid tiers). The free version limits you to 1,000 contacts and 1 pipeline, but for many small businesses that’s enough.

Salesforce Essentials (the small business plan) also supports one pipeline. The contact record is more flexible – you can add custom fields, formulas, and even validation rules. But the default experience feels cluttered. You’ll likely want to hide many standard fields and create your own layout.

One area where Salesforce shines: reporting. You can build custom reports with filters, groupings, and charts that HubSpot’s free/Starter plans can’t match. HubSpot’s reporting in lower tiers is basic – you get pre-built dashboards and limited customization.

Verdict: HubSpot for simplicity. Salesforce for reporting depth.

Email & Marketing Automation

HubSpot’s free CRM includes a basic email integration. The paid Marketing Hub (starting at $45/mo) adds email campaigns, forms, and a simple automation builder. For a small business running email newsletters or basic lead nurturing, this works well. The drag-and-drop email editor is easy, and you can track opens and clicks without any technical setup.

Salesforce’s email marketing requires either the Marketing Cloud (separate product, starts around $1,000/mo) or a third-party integration like Mailchimp. The native Sales Cloud has very limited marketing capabilities. If email marketing is important to you, HubSpot is the better choice unless you’re willing to pay for an add-on.

Verdict: HubSpot wins for marketing automation at small business scale.

Automation & Workflows

HubSpot offers workflow automation in its paid plans (starting at $45/mo for Marketing Hub). You can set triggers like "contact fills out a form" → "send email" → "create a task" → "assign to owner." It’s visual, easy to build, and great for common scenarios.

Salesforce has its own automation tools: Process Builder, Workflow Rules, and Flow. They are more powerful but also more complex. You can build multi-step processes with conditions, loops, and even call external APIs. However, the learning curve is steep. Many small businesses end up hiring a Salesforce admin to build their automations.

Verdict: HubSpot for built-in ease. Salesforce for unlimited possibilities.

Integrations

Both platforms have extensive app marketplaces. HubSpot’s App Marketplace includes free and paid integrations with Gmail, Outlook, Zoom, Slack, Shopify, WordPress, and hundreds of others. Many of these are free or low-cost.

Salesforce’s AppExchange is even larger, but most serious integrations come with a price tag. Connecting Salesforce to your email (Gmail) requires a third-party connector or a paid add-on. The upside: if you need to integrate with an obscure ERP or legacy system, Salesforce probably has a connector.

Verdict: Tie for breadth. HubSpot for cost.

Pricing: Where the Real Differences Show

Let’s talk money. HubSpot’s pricing is transparent and predictable. Salesforce’s pricing can feel like a game of whack-a-mole.

Note: HubSpot’s free CRM is genuinely usable. Salesforce has no free tier – only a 30-day trial.

But the real cost is in add-ons. Salesforce Essentials limits you to one pipeline and basic automation. To get multiple pipelines, you need Professional ($80/user/mo). To get advanced reporting, you need Enterprise. HubSpot’s Professional ($90/user/mo) includes more features out of the box: multiple pipelines, custom reporting, sequences, and meeting scheduling.

Also consider the cost of setup. Salesforce often requires a consultant for initial configuration, which can cost $1,000–$5,000. HubSpot can be set up by anyone in a day.

Verdict: HubSpot is cheaper for small businesses. Salesforce can become more expensive quickly.

Pros and Cons

HubSpot Pros

  • Free CRM is functional and usable
  • Easy to learn and set up
  • Includes email marketing and basic automation in paid plans
  • Clean, modern interface
  • Good customer support (paid plans)
  • Transparent pricing

HubSpot Cons

  • Can get expensive as you add hubs (Marketing, Sales, Service)
  • Limited customization in lower tiers
  • Reporting is basic without Professional plan
  • Contact and storage limits on free plan
  • No phone support on Starter plan

Salesforce Pros

  • Extremely customizable – can adapt to any business process
  • Powerful reporting and analytics
  • Massive ecosystem of apps and integrations
  • Scales to enterprise without migration
  • Strong mobile app with full features
  • Industry-specific solutions (healthcare, finance, etc.)

Salesforce Cons

  • Steep learning curve
  • Expensive when you add features and users
  • No free tier
  • Basic marketing automation requires extra purchase
  • Setup often needs professional help
  • Interface can feel cluttered

Final Verdict: Which CRM Should You Choose?

For most small businesses (under 25 employees, simple sales process, limited budget), HubSpot is the better choice. The free CRM lets you test everything risk-free. The paid plans are affordable and include features that Salesforce charges extra for. The ease of use means your team will actually adopt it, which is the #1 reason CRMs fail.

Pick Salesforce if:

  • You have a complex sales process with multiple deal types, custom objects, and approval chains.
  • You need deep reporting and dashboards that go beyond what HubSpot offers.
  • You plan to grow rapidly and want a single platform that scales without migration.
  • You have a dedicated admin or a consultant on retainer.

But be honest with yourself: most small businesses don’t need Salesforce’s power. They need something that works without a manual. HubSpot gives you that.

If you’re still unsure, start with HubSpot’s free CRM. Use it for a month. If you hit its limits (like needing more than one pipeline or custom reporting), then consider upgrading to HubSpot Professional or looking at Salesforce. That way, you’re not paying for complexity you don’t yet need.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate from HubSpot to Salesforce later?

Yes, but it’s not trivial. You can export your data from HubSpot as CSV and import into Salesforce. However, workflows, automations, and custom fields won’t transfer automatically. Plan for a few days of work or hire a consultant.

Is Salesforce overkill for a 5-person business?

Usually, yes. Unless you have very specific needs (e.g., complex quoting, custom objects, or integration with an ERP), HubSpot or even a simpler CRM like Pipedrive will serve you better.

Does HubSpot’s free plan include email tracking?

Yes, if you connect your Gmail or Outlook. You can see when a contact opens an email or clicks a link. But you’re limited to 1,000 contacts and 1 pipeline.

Which has better mobile apps?

Both are functional. HubSpot’s mobile app is simpler and great for quick updates. Salesforce’s mobile app can do almost everything the desktop version can, but it’s more cluttered.

Can I use both together?

Technically, yes, but it’s messy. Some companies use Salesforce as their backend and HubSpot for marketing. But keeping data in sync requires a third-party integration tool like Zapier, and it’s never perfect.

Do either offer a free trial?

HubSpot has a free tier (no trial needed). Salesforce offers a 30-day free trial of Essentials or Professional.

Which CRM is better for ecommerce?

HubSpot integrates natively with Shopify, WooCommerce, and BigCommerce. Salesforce requires an add-on (Salesforce Commerce Cloud is a separate product). For a small ecommerce business, HubSpot is simpler.
