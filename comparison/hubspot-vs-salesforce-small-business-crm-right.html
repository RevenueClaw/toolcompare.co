Hubspot vs Salesforce for Small Business: Which CRM Is Right for You?

Choosing a CRM is one of those decisions that can either Make your sales process feel effortless or turn it into a daily headache. HubSpot and Salesforce are the two giants, but they take very different approaches — especially when you're a small business with a limited budget and a small team.

I've spent weeks testing both platforms with a mock small business setup: a team of five, a few hundred contacts, and a simple sales pipeline. Here’s what I found.

What Each CRM Does Well (and Where They Fall Short)

HubSpot CRM

HubSpot starts as a free CRM that's surprisingly capable. You get contact management, deal tracking, email integration, and even a basic chatbot — all without paying a cent. The interface is clean, modern, and dead simple to navigate. If you've ever used Gmail or Slack, you'll feel right at home.

The catch: HubSpot’s free tier is generous, but it locks many useful features behind its paid plans. Want to automate follow-up emails? That's a paid feature. Need custom reporting? Paid. And the paid plans aren't cheap.

Best for: Teams that want a CRM they can start using in an afternoon and don’t need complex customizations.

Salesforce CRM

Salesforce is the industry heavyweight. It can handle millions of records, complex workflows, and integrations with almost anything. But out of the box, it feels like you’re trying to fly a spaceship with no training manual.

The “Starter” suite starts at $25/user/month, which sounds reasonable. But you’ll quickly discover that many basics — like email integration or reporting — require add-ons or a higher tier. The learning curve is real. Expect to spend days (or weeks) setting things up properly.

Best for: Businesses that have outgrown simpler tools and need deep customization, or that plan to scale quickly.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

Contact Management

Both CRMs let you store contacts, log interactions, and view a timeline of emails and calls. HubSpot’s contact records feel more intuitive — you see a clear activity feed, associated deals, and notes in one scrollable pane. Salesforce’s record layout is more configurable but can look cluttered until you customize it.

Winner: HubSpot for simplicity. Salesforce if you need custom fields and complex relationships.

Email Integration

HubSpot connects to Gmail and Outlook with a free sidebar widget. You can log emails automatically, track opens, and use templates. Salesforce requires the "Salesforce for Outlook" plugin or the "Email Integration" add-on (often extra cost). The free Starter plan only gives you basic send-from-Salesforce capabilities.

Winner: HubSpot, especially for small teams that live in email.

Sales Automation

HubSpot’s “Sequences” let you send a series of follow-up emails based on triggers like a contact opening a previous email. This is a paid feature (Starter plan). Salesforce offers “Automation” in Starter (5 rules) and more in Essentials. Both are functional, but HubSpot’s interface is easier for non-techies.

Winner: Tie for basic automation. HubSpot wins on ease of use.

Reporting & Dashboards

HubSpot’s free tier gives you basic dashboards (deal stage, activity). For custom reports, you need the Professional plan at $800/month. Salesforce’s Essentials plan has report types but limited customization. To get real analytics, you’ll need at least the Professional plan, which is $80/user/month.

Winner: Neither is great for small budgets. Both push you to higher tiers for meaningful reporting.

AI & Smart Features

HubSpot includes free AI tools like content suggestions and predictive lead scoring in higher plans. Salesforce’s Einstein AI is a separate add-on costing $50/user/month. For most small businesses, you won't need AI until you’re managing hundreds of leads.

Winner: HubSpot, because you get some AI without extra cost.

Pricing: What You'll Actually Pay

Small businesses usually look at the first paid tier. Let’s break down the real cost for a 5-person team.

HubSpot Starter – $50/month (up to 5 users). That’s $600/year. You get email tracking, basic automation, and pipelines. But you’re capped at 1,000 contacts for automation triggers.

Salesforce Starter – $25/user/month × 5 = $125/month = $1,500/year. That includes pipelines, email integration (basic), and 10,000 contact storage. But you’ll likely need add-ons for reporting ($30/user) and email automation ($50/user) — quickly pushing you past $200/month.

The hidden costs: Salesforce charges for most integrations through AppExchange. HubSpot’s App Marketplace has many free integrations, but premium ones also cost.

Winner for price: HubSpot, especially if you can stay on the free plan or Starter. Salesforce gets expensive fast.

Pros and Cons

HubSpot Pros

  • Free CRM that actually works well
  • Very easy to learn and use
  • Clean, modern interface
  • Good email integration out of the box
  • Active community and free training

HubSpot Cons

  • Paid features are expensive (Professional plan jumps to $800/month)
  • Limited customization on lower plans
  • Can feel “bloated” if you don’t use all the marketing features
  • Reporting is weak until you pay up

Salesforce Pros

  • Extremely powerful and customizable
  • Scales to enterprise with no limit
  • Massive ecosystem of apps and integrations
  • Strong automation and workflow engine
  • Good for complex sales processes

Salesforce Cons

  • Steep learning curve
  • Expensive when you add all features
  • Interface can be overwhelming
  • Basic features like email sync are paywalled
  • Setup takes time and often needs a consultant

Who Should Choose HubSpot?

Pick HubSpot if:

  • You have fewer than 10 employees and a simple sales process.
  • You want a CRM you can start using today without a training course.
  • You prefer an all-in-one platform (CRM + marketing + service) even if it costs more later.
  • You care about a clean, modern user experience.

Who Should Choose Salesforce?

Pick Salesforce if:

  • You have a complex sales process with multiple stakeholders and approval chains.
  • You plan to grow quickly and need a system that can handle 100+ users.
  • You have the time (or budget) to invest in proper setup and training.
  • You need deep customization (custom objects, workflows, integrations).

The Verdict

For most small businesses, HubSpot is the better choice. It gives you a functional free CRM, a much lower starting price, and an interface that doesn’t require a manual. You can start selling in an hour, not a week.

Salesforce is more powerful, but that power comes with complexity and cost that most small teams don’t need. Unless you already have a dedicated CRM admin or a very specific workflow requirement, HubSpot will serve you better — and save you money.

Our pick: HubSpot CRM (free or Starter plan). If you outgrow it, you can always migrate to Salesforce later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HubSpot CRM truly free?

Yes, the free plan includes contact management, deal tracking, email integration, and live chat. No credit card required. You're limited to 1 million contacts, which is plenty for most small businesses.

Can I import data from Excel into HubSpot or Salesforce?

Both allow CSV imports. HubSpot’s import wizard is simpler and includes automatic field mapping. Salesforce’s import tool works but can be finicky with custom fields.

Which CRM is easier to set up?

HubSpot. You can be up and running in 30 minutes. Salesforce takes at least a few days to configure properly, and you might need to watch tutorials or hire a consultant.

Do I need to pay for email tracking?

HubSpot includes email tracking (opens, clicks) in the free plan. Salesforce’s Starter plan includes basic tracking; more advanced features require an add-on.

Can I switch from HubSpot to Salesforce later?

Yes. Both offer export tools. You can export contacts, deals, and notes as CSV. Migrating workflows and automation is more complex, but doable with tools like Import2 or a consultant.

Which CRM has better mobile apps?

Both have solid mobile apps. HubSpot’s app feels more polished, while Salesforce’s app is more powerful but can be slow on older devices.

What if I need marketing features like email campaigns?

HubSpot includes basic email marketing in the free plan (up to 2,000 emails/day). Salesforce requires Marketing Cloud, which is a separate high-cost product. For small businesses, HubSpot’s all-in-one approach is better.

Still unsure? Both offer free trials. Sign up for HubSpot’s free CRM first, and if you hit its limits, try Salesforce’s 30-day trial. You’ll quickly see which one fits your workflow.
