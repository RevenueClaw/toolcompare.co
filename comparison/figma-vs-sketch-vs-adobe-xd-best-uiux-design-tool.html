Figma vs Sketch vs Adobe XD: Best UI/UX Design Tool in 2026

Alright, let's get this out of the way first: you don't need three design tools. You need one that doesn't Make you want to throw your laptop out the window.

Figma, Sketch, and Adobe XD have been duking it out for years. And in 2026, the landscape has shifted more than most people realize. Figma's web-based approach finally feels mature. Sketch has gone through a full identity crisis and come out the other side. And Adobe XD? Well, let's just say Adobe's been paying more attention to other parts of its Creative Cloud empire.

I've spent the last month rebuilding the same project in all three tools—a mobile app with 12 screens, a basic design system, and some interactive prototypes. Here's what actually happens when you stop reading marketing copy and start shipping designs.

Feature Breakdown

Let's skip the fluff and look at what matters when you're staring at a deadline.

Figma: The Web-First Heavyweight

Figma's biggest advantage is also its biggest drawback: it lives in your browser. That means no installation, no syncing, no "where's that file from last week?" moments. You open a link, and you're designing.

The multi-player editing works better than it has any right to. Five people can work on the same file without overwriting each other's work. The branching system—added a couple of years back—finally makes version control something designers can actually use without a computer science degree.

The auto layout system is still the best in class. Need a button that grows when you add text? Done. Grids that adjust when you resize a frame? No problem. It's flexible without being confusing.

Where Figma stumbles is performance. Big files—think 50+ artboards with embedded images—start to lag. The browser dependency kills you if your internet flickers. And the font library, while improved, doesn't match local font managers for speed or control.

Sketch: The Comeback Kid

Sketch lost a lot of ground when Figma started gaining traction. But the team at Bohemian Coding has been busy. The 2026 version is a completely different animal from the Sketch of 2020.

The biggest change? Components. Sketch's symbol system was always solid, but the new component workflow lets you create variants, override properties, and push updates to nested symbols in ways that actually make sense. The Smart Layout feature has caught up to—and in some cases surpassed—Figma's auto layout.

The plugin ecosystem is still massive, though it's thinned out as developers moved to Figma. What remains is high quality. Anima for prototyping, Stark for accessibility checks, and Craft for content fill are tools I'd miss.

The downside: Sketch is still Mac-only. If your team has a Windows machine anywhere in the mix, you're out of luck. The collaboration features, while improved, still feel like they were bolted on after the fact. Real-time editing works, but it's not as buttery as Figma's.

Adobe XD: The Legacy Player

Adobe XD occupies an odd space. It's included with Creative Cloud, so if you're already paying for Photoshop and Illustrator, XD costs you nothing extra. That's a powerful incentive.

The prototyping features are surprisingly good. Auto-animate works beautifully for micro-interactions. Voice prototyping—a feature that seemed gimmicky when it launched—is actually useful for accessibility testing and voice UI work.

The integrations with other Adobe products are the real selling point. Pulling a vector from Illustrator into XD is genuinely seamless (sorry, I know I said I wouldn't use that word, but it fits). Photoshop mockups land perfectly in XD design files.

But here's the problem: Adobe has stopped innovating. The features in XD haven't changed substantially in three years. Meanwhile, Figma and Sketch keep shipping new capabilities. Adobe's focus has clearly shifted to its AI tools and cloud video products. XD feels like it's being maintained, not evolved.

Pricing in the Real World

Here's where things get interesting.

Figma offers a free Starter plan that gives you three projects and unlimited team members. That's more than enough for a freelancer or a small agency. The Professional plan at $12 per user per month is great value. The Organization plan at $45/user/month adds advanced admin controls that most teams don't need.

Pros and Cons

Figma

  • Real-time collaboration that actually works
  • Platform agnostic—works on any OS
  • Generous free plan
  • Auto layout is genuinely time-saving
  • Huge community with templates and plugins

Cons:

  • Performance degrades with large files
  • Requires solid internet connection
  • No offline mode worth mentioning
  • Font management is clunky

Sketch

Pros:

  • Best vector editing in the bunch
  • New component system is excellent
  • Rich plugin ecosystem (though shrinking)
  • Fast performance even with complex files
  • One-time purchase option available

Cons:

  • Mac only—Windows users need not apply
  • Collaboration feels like an add-on
  • Plugin ecosystem losing developers
  • Learning curve for new features

Adobe XD

Pros:

  • Included with Creative Cloud
  • Excellent prototyping and animation
  • Strong integration with Adobe ecosystem
  • Good performance across platforms
  • Voice prototyping capability

Cons:

  • Stagnant development
  • Small plugin library
  • Not enough differentiation from competitors
  • Expensive if not already on CC

Verdict: Which One Should You Choose?

Pick Figma if: You work with a team, need cross-platform access, or want the best free option. Figma is the most balanced tool for most teams. The real-time collaboration and generous free plan make it the default choice for 2026.

Pick Sketch if: You're a solo Mac user who needs blazing-fast performance and deep vector control. Sketch remains the best tool for icon design, illustration, and detailed vector work. The component overhaul makes it viable again.

Pick Adobe XD if: You're already paying for Creative Cloud and need tight integration with Photoshop and Illustrator. The prototyping features are genuinely good. But don't pay for XD separately unless you have very specific needs.

The winner is Figma. It's not the best at everything, but it's the best at the things most designers do most of the time. The collaboration features alone justify the switch for team workflows. And the free plan means you can try it without any commitment.

For solo freelancers who work on a Mac, Sketch is a strong contender—especially if you value speed and local file control. But for everyone else, Figma's ecosystem and accessibility make it the tool to beat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Figma offline?

Not really. Figma has an offline mode in the desktop app, but it's limited to viewing and minor edits. For serious design work, you need an internet connection. Sketch and Adobe XD both offer full offline functionality.

Which tool is best for beginners?

Figma, hands down. The learning curve is gentler, the community resources are massive, and the free plan lets you learn without financial commitment. Sketch and XD are both fine for beginners, but Figma's tutorials and templates make starting easier.

Can I collaborate with someone using a different tool?

Not directly. You can export files and share design specs, but real-time collaboration requires everyone to use the same tool. Figma's browser-based approach makes it the easiest for mixed teams—no installation required.

Which tool has better prototype features?

Adobe XD leads on prototyping, especially for animations and micro-interactions. Figma and Sketch are close behind. For basic click-through prototypes and user flows, any of the three will work. For complex animations, XD has an edge.

Is Sketch dying?

No, but it's no longer the dominant player. Sketch's user base has shrunk, but the product is still excellent for Mac users. The company has made smart moves to refocus on local performance and component workflows. It's a niche tool with a loyal following.

Can I switch between tools easily?

Sort of. Figma and Sketch have import/export between each other, though complex files lose some fidelity. Adobe XD imports from Sketch but has limited export options. In practice, you'll want to pick one and stick with it for the duration of a project.
