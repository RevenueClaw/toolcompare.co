Norton vs NordVPN vs ExpressVPN on Black Friday 2026: Real Savings Compared

Black Friday 2026 lands on November 27, with Cyber Monday close behind on November 30. Which means right now your inbox is filling up with VPN emails promising 80% off, "3 months free," and countdown timers that somehow reset every time you refresh the page.

Here's the thing nobody puts in those emails: the discount percentage is the least useful number on the page. What actually matters is your total cost over the full term, what the price jumps to on renewal, and whether you're buying a VPN or a bundle you didn't ask for.

So I did the math on the three names you're most likely comparing — Norton, NordVPN, and ExpressVPN — using their typical Black Friday pricing patterns. One of them is a genuinely great deal. One is a decent deal wrapped in a security suite. And one is the best product of the three that you should still think twice about at full price.

Let me show you the numbers.

First, a Quick Reality Check on VPN Pricing

All three of these companies run the same basic playbook: big discount on the first term, higher price when it renews. That's not a scam, but it is the single most important thing to understand before you enter a credit card number.

A "2-year plan for $2.99/month" is not a $2.99/month product. It's a $71.76 upfront payment for 24 months, after which you'll be charged somewhere between $60 and $140 per year unless you cancel or renegotiate.

I'm using typical Black Friday pricing patterns below rather than live prices, because these deals shift daily through November. Treat the numbers as close approximations and verify on the actual checkout page before you buy. The relationships between the prices — which is cheapest, which has the worst renewal — hold up year after year.

What You're Actually Buying

This matters more than people expect, because these three products aren't really the same category.

Norton Secure VPN is a standalone VPN, but its real value shows up inside Norton 360, the antivirus suite that bundles VPN, password manager, dark web monitoring, and cloud backup. Norton is owned by Gen Digital, a US-based company that also owns Avast, AVIRA, and LifeLock. If you want one subscription that covers malware and encrypted browsing, this is the pitch.

NordVPN is a dedicated VPN from Nord Security, based in Panama. It's the biggest independent player in the space and also sells NordPass (password manager) and NordLocker (encrypted storage). NordVPN is the one people mean when they say "just get a VPN."

ExpressVPN is the premium-priced option, based in the British Virgin Islands and owned by Kape Technologies — which also owns CyberGhost, Private Internet Access, and ZenMate. That consolidation is worth knowing about if corporate ownership matters to you.

Feature Breakdown: Where They Actually Differ

Server Network and Locations

NordVPN runs roughly 7,000+ servers across 118 countries. ExpressVPN sits around 3,000 servers in 105 countries. Norton Secure VPN is the outlier — around 2,000 servers in about 30 countries.

That gap matters if you travel or need a specific country for streaming or work. Thirty countries covers the big ones, but it's a noticeably thinner map than the other two.

Speed and Protocol

  • NordVPN uses NordLynx, its WireGuard-based protocol. In most independent speed tests it lands at or near the top, typically losing only a small percentage of your base connection.
  • ExpressVPN developed Lightway, its own protocol, and it's excellent for fast reconnects — useful on mobile when you're hopping between Wi-Fi and cellular.
  • Norton Secure VPN uses a WireGuard-based protocol too, but it consistently tests slower than the other two in third-party benchmarks. Not unusable, just not the one you pick if speed is your priority.

Security and Privacy Track Record

This is where the comparison gets lopsided.

NordVPN and ExpressVPN have both submitted to repeated independent audits of their no-logs claims and server infrastructure — Deloitte and Cure53 for Nord, Cure53 and F-Secure for Express. Both use RAM-only server setups, meaning a server reboot wipes its data.

Norton Secure VPN states a no-logs policy, and Gen Digital publishes transparency reports, but the VPN product itself has a much shorter public audit history than the other two. It's not a red flag exactly. It's just less verification for a similar price.

Extras

  • Norton 360 Deluxe includes antivirus, a password manager, dark web monitoring, 50GB cloud backup, and parental controls. If you're paying for antivirus separately today, this changes the math significantly.

  • ExpressVPN includes a password manager, a tracker/ad blocker, and its Keys password tool. Solid, but less generous than either alternative.

The Savings Math, Worked Out

NordVPN Basic, 2-year Black Friday deal: roughly $74–$97 upfront versus $311.76 if you paid monthly for 24 months. That's a real saving of $215–$238. This is the biggest genuine discount in the group.

ExpressVPN, 15-month deal: about $99.95 versus $194.25 at the monthly rate. You save roughly $94. Good, but you're paying a premium per month for it.

Norton 360 Deluxe: $39.99 for year one versus $119.99 on renewal. That's a 67% discount — the largest percentage in the table. But it's the renewal that bites, and it bites hard.

That last point deserves its own section.

The Renewal Trap (Read This Before You Buy Anything)

Norton's renewal pricing is the most aggressive of the three. A $39.99 first year becomes $119.99 in year two if you do nothing. That's a 200% increase, and it's the single most common complaint in Norton reviews.

NordVPN renews higher too — typically landing in the $60–$100/year range after a promotional term — but the jump is less severe. ExpressVPN's renewal is the gentlest of the three, though you're starting from a higher base.

The fix is simple and takes two minutes: set a calendar reminder 30 days before your term ends. Every one of these companies will offer you a retention discount if you go to cancel. I've never once paid full renewal on a VPN, and you don't have to either.

Also worth knowing: Norton offers a 60-day money-back guarantee. NordVPN and ExpressVPN both offer 30 days. If you're unsure, Norton gives you the longest Runway to change your mind.
