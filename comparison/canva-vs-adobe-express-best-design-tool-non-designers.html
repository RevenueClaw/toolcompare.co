So you need to create graphics for your business, but you can barely tell a layer from a text box. Trust me, I've been there. Two tools claim they can Make you look like a pro without the learning curve: Canva and Adobe Express (formerly Adobe Spark).

I've spent the past month building actual projects in both—social media posts, flyers, presentations, even a quick logo mockup. Here's what I found.

The 30-Second Verdict

If you need one answer right now: Canva wins for 90% of non-designers. It's cheaper, has more templates, and its AI features actually work. But Adobe Express has a few tricks up its sleeve, especially if you already use other Adobe products.

That said, let's dig into the details so you can make the right call for your specific situation.

Detailed Feature Breakdown

Templates: Canva Crushes It

This is the biggest difference. Canva has over 250,000 templates. Adobe Express has maybe 20,000. And it's not just about quantity—Canva's templates are generally more modern and better designed.

For example, I created an Instagram story for a coffee shop. Canva had 50+ coffee-related templates. Adobe Express had maybe 8, and three of them looked like they were designed in 2019.

That said, Adobe Express templates are more "brand-consistent" out of the box. They use Adobe Fonts and have better typography by default. If you're working on a brand guide, it's easier to keep things cohesive in Express.

Winner: Canva – Unless you need strict brand consistency, Canva's template library is unbeatable.

AI Features: Close, But Different

Both tools now have AI features, but they work differently.

Canva Magic Studio includes:

  • Magic Media: Text-to-image (generates images from descriptions)
  • Magic Eraser: Remove objects from photos
  • Magic Expand: Extend image backgrounds
  • Background Remover: AI-powered (works well ~85% of the time)

Adobe Express with Firefly includes:

  • Text-to-Image: Also generates images, but uses Adobe's Firefly engine
  • Generative Fill: Fill selected areas with AI-generated content
  • Text Effects: Apply textures and styles to text
  • Remove Background: Similar to Canva, but sometimes cleaner on complex edges

I tested both with the same prompt: "a latte art swan on coffee foam, photorealistic."

Canva's result was decent—a bit soft, but usable. Adobe Express's Firefly version looked more realistic, with better lighting and detail.

For background removal on a photo of a curly-haired person, Adobe Express handled the hair edges better. Canva left a slight halo.

Winner: Adobe Express – Firefly is genuinely better for image generation. But honestly, both are improving fast.

Ease of Use: Both Are Beginner-Friendly

Here's the thing: both tools are drag-and-drop simple. You don't need any design experience to produce something that won't embarrass you.

Canva has a slightly cleaner interface. Everything is where you'd expect it. Adobe Express feels a bit cluttered sometimes—there are too many buttons and options crowded into the left sidebar.

But Adobe Express wins on one thing: Keyboard shortcuts. If you're used to Adobe software, you'll feel at home. Canva relies more on clicking through menus.

Winner: Canva – Slightly less intimidating for total beginners.

Collaboration: Canva Takes This

Canva lets you share designs, leave comments, and work in real-time with team members. Their "Brand Kit" feature (on Pro) lets you set brand colors, fonts, and logos that everyone on your team can access.

Adobe Express has collaboration features too, but they're more basic. You can share links and get feedback, but real-time co-editing isn't as smooth. And the brand tools require Creative Cloud subscriptions.

Winner: Canva – Especially for small teams.

Stock Assets: Adobe Has the Library, But You Pay

Canva Pro gives you access to 1 million+ stock photos, videos, and elements. That's plenty for most people. But Adobe Express Premium unlocks 200 million+ assets from Adobe Stock.

The catch? Adobe Stock assets are individually priced beyond what's included. So you might find the perfect photo, but need to pay extra to download it without watermark.

Canva's stock is all-you-can-eat on Pro. Simpler, more budget-friendly.

Winner: Canva – Unless you need very specific, high-end stock photography.

When to Choose Canva

Choose Canva if:

  • You're a complete beginner
  • You need tons of templates (social media, presentations, flyers, resumes)
  • You work with a team
  • You want all-in-one stock access
  • You're on a budget

Canva is NOT ideal if:

  • You need advanced vector editing (use Illustrator instead)
  • You rely on Adobe-specific fonts or assets
  • You want the absolute best AI image generation

When to Choose Adobe Express

Choose Adobe Express if:

  • You already pay for Adobe Creative Cloud
  • You need deep Photoshop/Illustrator integration
  • You want better AI image generation
  • You work with strict brand guidelines
  • You need cleaner background removal

Adobe Express is NOT ideal if:

  • You don't use other Adobe products
  • You want the widest template selection
  • You need robust collaboration tools
  • You're a solo user on a tight budget

Real-World Test: Creating a Facebook Ad

I created a Facebook ad for a fake bakery using both tools. Here's how they compared:

Canva:

  • Template found in 30 seconds
  • Customized in 8 minutes
  • Exported as high-res PNG
  • Total time: ~10 minutes

Adobe Express:

  • Template found in 2 minutes (fewer options)
  • Customized in 12 minutes (more manual adjustments needed)
  • Exported as PNG
  • Total time: ~15 minutes

Both results looked professional. Canva's was faster, but Express's typography was slightly better.

FAQ

Is Canva better than Adobe Express?

For most people, yes. Canva has a larger template library, better collaboration, and a more generous free tier. Adobe Express is better only if you need Adobe-specific features or superior AI.

Can I use Canva for professional graphic design?

Absolutely. Canva is used by many businesses for social media graphics, presentations, flyers, and even basic branding. It's not a substitute for Photoshop for heavy photo editing, but for most design needs, it's more than enough.

Is Adobe Express free?

Adobe Express has a free tier, but it's very limited—you get few templates, 2GB of storage, and exports have watermarks. The Premium plan is $10/month.

Which is better for teams?

Canva. Their collaboration features are more mature, real-time co-editing works well, and brand kits are included in the Pro plan. Adobe Express team features are still catching up.

Can I use Adobe Express without a Creative Cloud subscription?

Yes, you can use it standalone. But you'll miss out on integration features like accessing Adobe Fonts or syncing with Photoshop.

Which has better AI features?

Adobe Express with Firefly has better image generation. Canva's Magic Studio is more versatile for different tasks (like background removal and text effects), but Firefly generates more realistic images.

Do either of these replace Photoshop?

No. Both are for quick, template-based design. For serious photo editing, retouching, or illustration, you still need Photoshop or Illustrator.

Can I export SVG files?

Canva Pro exports SVG. Adobe Express Premium also exports SVG.

Is there a student discount?

Canva Pro offers a discount for students (check current pricing). Adobe Express doesn't have a specific student plan, but students get discounts on Creative Cloud.

Which one is better for making logos?

Neither, honestly. Use Canva or Express for quick logo mockups, but for a professional logo, hire a designer or use vector software like Illustrator or Affinity Designer.
