Let's be honest: you didn't start your business to sit in back-to-back Zoom calls, squinting at a shared screen, and hoping someone remembers the action items. Yet here we are.

The average professional spends nearly 15 hours a week in meetings. That's almost two full workdays. And the worst part? Most of that time is spent doing things an AI could handle — transcribing, summarizing, tracking decisions, and chasing follow-ups.

I've spent the last month testing 20+ AI meeting assistants across different use cases. Some are brilliant. Some are glorified voice recorders with a pretty interface. Below are the seven that genuinely cut my meeting time in half — and the one I'd actually pay for out of pocket.

What to Look for in an AI Meeting Assistant

Before we get to the list, here's the quick checklist I used while testing. Skip this section if you're in a hurry, but it'll save you from buyer's remorse:

  • Transcription accuracy — Can it handle accents, crosstalk, and technical jargon?
  • Action item detection — Does it just transcribe, or does it understand what needs to happen next?
  • Integration depth — Does it plug into your CRM, project management tool, and calendar?
  • Searchability — Can you find "the conversation about the Q3 budget" without scrubbing through audio?
  • Pricing model — Per user? Per meeting? Unlimited? Watch out for hidden caps.

The 7 Best AI Meeting Assistants in 2026

1. Fireflies.ai — Best Overall for Most Teams

Price: Free plan (unlimited transcription, 3 seats) | Pro: $18/seat/month | Business: $29/seat/month

Fireflies has been around for a while, and it shows. The transcription accuracy is excellent — I tested it with a heavy Indian accent and a fast-talking sales rep talking over each other, and it only missed a handful of words.

What sets Fireflies apart is the topic tracking and sentiment analysis. It doesn't just transcribe; it tags conversations by topic (pricing, objections, next steps) and flags whether the tone was positive or negative. For sales teams, this is gold. You can search your entire meeting history by keyword and instantly pull up every mention of "budget" across all your calls.

The free plan is genuinely usable — unlimited transcription, but you only get 800 minutes of storage. That's roughly 13 hours of meetings. For a solo freelancer, that might be enough. For a team, you'll need the Pro plan.

Who it's for: Small to mid-sized teams that want a dependable, all-in-one solution without learning a new workflow.

2. Otter.ai — Best for Meeting Productivity Tools Integration

Price: Free (300 min/month) | Pro: $16.99/month | Business: $30/user/month

Otter is probably the most recognizable name in AI note-taking, and for good reason. It's the fastest at generating live summaries — you can watch the notes populate in real-time during the call. That's a nice party trick, but the real value is in the Meeting Gems feature (yes, that's what they call it). After your call, Otter automatically pulls out action items, decisions, and key questions — no prompting required.

Where Otter really shines is its native integration with Google Meet and Zoom. It joins the call automatically if you've connected your calendar. No bots to invite, no "Otter is now recording" awkwardness. It just shows up.

The downside? The free plan is stingy — 300 minutes per month is about 5 hours. And the "action item detection" isn't as smart as Fireflies. It'll flag things that sound like tasks, but sometimes it misses the context (e.g., who's responsible for what).

Who it's for: Google Workspace users who want a frictionless, set-and-forget solution.

3. Fathom — Best Free Tier for Sales Teams

Price: Free forever (unlimited meetings) | Pro: $19/user/month | Team: $29/user/month

Fathom is the underdog that's quietly eating Otter's lunch. The free plan is unlimited — no caps on meeting minutes, no storage limits. That alone makes it worth a look.

But here's the thing: Fathom was built specifically for sales calls. It integrates directly with Salesforce, HubSpot, and Slack. When a call ends, it can automatically log the meeting, update the opportunity stage, and post a summary to the relevant Slack channel. That's the kind of automation that saves you 30 minutes per call, not just 5.

The transcription quality is solid, but the real differentiator is the live coaching feature. During a call, Fathom can display real-time suggestions — "you asked 3 questions in a row, slow down" or "the prospect mentioned a budget constraint, dig deeper." It's like having a sales manager whispering in your ear.

Who it's for: Sales teams and account executives who live in their CRM.

4. tl;dv — Best for Zoom-First Teams

Price: Free (40 min/meeting) | Pro: $20/user/month

If you're a Zoom shop, tl;dv (that's "too long; didn't view") is worth a hard look. It's built natively for Zoom, so the transcription and recording quality is excellent — better than Otter's Zoom integration in my testing.

The standout feature is timestamped bookmarks. You can click a button during the call to mark a moment, and tl;dv creates a shareable link to that exact point in the recording. No more "let me find the part where we discussed the pricing" — you just send the link.

It also does speaker identification well, which matters when you have 10 people on a call and need to know who said what. The free plan is limited to 40-minute meetings, which is frustrating if you have longer strategy sessions. But for most sales and client calls, that's fine.

Who it's for: Zoom power users who want granular control over meeting recordings and sharing.

5. Krisp — Best for Audio Quality, Not Notes

Price: Free (60 min/day) | Pro: $8/month

Krisp is a bit of a different beast. It's primarily an audio enhancement tool — it removes background noise, echo, and crosstalk in real-time. If you've ever been on a call where someone's dog won't stop barking, you know the pain Krisp solves.

But here's the twist: Krisp now includes AI meeting notes as part of its package. The transcription is decent, but the real value is the noise cancellation feeds into better AI understanding. When everyone sounds crisp, the AI makes fewer mistakes.

The notes feature is basic compared to Fireflies or Otter — no action item detection, no CRM integration. This is a "meeting assistant" in the loosest sense. But if your problem is sound quality more than note-taking, Krisp is the best $8/month you'll spend.

Who it's for: Remote workers in noisy environments or anyone who wants crystal-clear audio on every call.

6. Sembly.ai — Best for Project Management Integration

Price: Free (240 min/month) | Pro: $12/user/month

Sembly flies under the radar compared to the big names, but it has one killer feature: direct integration with Asana, Trello, and Monday.com. When an action item is identified in a meeting, Sembly can automatically create a task card with the assignee and due date. No manual entry, no copy-paste.

That's a huge time-saver for teams that live in project management tools. The transcription is solid, and the summaries are organized by "decisions," "action items," and "risks" — which is more structured than most competitors.

The downside? The free plan is limited to 240 minutes per month (4 hours), and the interface feels a bit dated. It also doesn't handle crosstalk as well as Fireflies. But if your bottleneck is task management rather than note-taking, Sembly is a strong pick.

Who it's for: Teams that live in Asana/Trello and want meetings to feed directly into their workflow.

7. Avoma — Best for Customer-Facing Teams

Price: Free (3 meetings/month) | Starter: $39/user/month

Avoma is the enterprise-grade option. It's more expensive, but it does things the others can't. For starters, it combines meeting recording, AI notes, and a conversation intelligence dashboard in one platform. You can see talk-to-listen ratios, pace, and filler word usage across your entire team.

The AI summaries are the most detailed of any tool I tested — they include the full transcript, a condensed summary, action items, and even a "tone analysis" that flags if the customer sounded frustrated or happy.

The catch? The free plan is practically a demo (3 meetings per month), and the paid plans start at $39/user/month. That's steep for a solo freelancer. But for customer success and sales teams that need deep insights, Avoma pays for itself.

Who it's for: Customer success managers, sales leaders, and teams that need conversation analytics.

How I Tested These Tools

I ran each tool through the same gauntlet: a 45-minute sales call with heavy back-and-forth, a 30-minute internal team meeting with multiple speakers talking over each other, and a 15-minute one-on-one with technical jargon.

I evaluated transcription accuracy, action item detection, and integration ease. I also checked how long it took to set up each tool — because if it takes 20 minutes to configure, it's not saving you time.

The Verdict: Which One Should You Pick?

Here's my honest take after a month of testing:

If you're a solo freelancer or small team: Go with Fathom. The unlimited free plan is a no-brainer, and the CRM integrations are a bonus if you ever need them.

If you're a sales team: Fireflies.ai is the winner. The topic tracking and search functionality alone justify the $18/month per seat. You'll spend less time scrubbing calls and more time closing deals.

If you're a Google Workspace shop: Otter.ai is the most frictionless option. The native Meet integration means you never have to think about it — it just works.

If you're a Zoom power user: tl;dv is your best bet. The timestamped bookmarks are a killer feature.

If you're a customer success leader: Avoma is worth the premium price for the conversation intelligence dashboard.

But if I had to pick one winner for the majority of people reading this? Fireflies.ai. It hits the sweet spot of accuracy, features, and price. The free plan is usable, the paid plan is reasonable, and it doesn't force you into a specific ecosystem.

FAQ

Are AI meeting assistants secure?

Most reputable tools are SOC 2 Type II compliant and offer encryption at rest and in transit. Fireflies, Otter, and Avoma all have enterprise-grade security. Just check their privacy policies — some tools use your data to train their AI models, which might be a dealbreaker for sensitive client info.

Can AI meeting assistants join calls automatically?

Yes, most of them can. Otter and Fathom integrate directly with Google Meet and Zoom, so they'll join automatically if you've connected your calendar. Fireflies requires you to invite the bot, but you can set up auto-join via the calendar integration.

Do they work with Microsoft Teams?

Yes, most of them do. Fireflies, Otter, and Avoma all have native Teams integrations. tl;dv is Zoom-focused but does support Teams. Krisp works across all platforms since it operates at the audio driver level.

What's the difference between transcription and AI notes?

Transcription is the raw text of everything said in the meeting. AI notes are the interpreted version — summaries, action items, decisions, and key questions. A good meeting assistant does both, but the quality of the AI notes is what separates the tools.

Can I use these tools for non-English meetings?

Most support multiple languages. Fireflies supports 69+ languages, Otter supports 10+ (including Spanish, French, and German), and Avoma supports 20+. If you regularly meet with international clients, check language support before committing.

Do I need a paid plan?

Not necessarily. Fathom's free plan is unlimited, and Fireflies' free plan is generous. If you have fewer than 5 hours of meetings per week, you can probably get by with free tiers. But if you want CRM integration, advanced search, or conversation analytics, you'll need to pay.

Bottom Line

AI meeting assistants are one of the few tools that genuinely pay for themselves. If you're in 10+ hours of meetings a week, even a $20/month subscription saves you several hours of note-taking and follow-up. That's a return on investment most software can't match.

Start with a free plan, test it on your real meetings, and see if it actually saves you time. If it does, upgrade. If it doesn't, try another one. The beauty of this category is that most tools have usable free tiers — there's no excuse not to try one today.
