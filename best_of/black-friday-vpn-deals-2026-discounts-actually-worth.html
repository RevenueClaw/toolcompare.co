Black Friday VPN Deals 2026: Which Discounts Are Actually Worth It

Every November, the same ritual plays out. VPN providers plaster "82% OFF" across their homepage, add a countdown timer, and hope you don't pull out a calculator. Most of those percentages are measured against a monthly rate that almost nobody actually pays. Some of the deals are recycled from October. A few of the banners will still be running in February.

That doesn't mean every Black Friday VPN deal is fake. A handful genuinely save you real money — usually somewhere between $40 and $90 over the life of a two-year plan, and considerably more if the alternative is paying month to month. The trick is knowing which number to look at, and which numbers exist purely to Make the discount look bigger.

This guide covers what's actually on offer for Black Friday 2026, which discounts hold up under scrutiny, and which provider makes sense depending on what you need a VPN for. Prices move around during sale season, so treat the figures below as the typical range rather than a locked-in quote — but the structure of these deals doesn't change much year to year, and that's what matters for your decision.

The Only Number That Matters

Ignore the percentage. Ignore the "was" price. Ignore the countdown timer, which resets every 24 hours anyway.

The number you want is the effective monthly cost on the term you'd actually commit to, multiplied by the number of months, compared against what you'd pay for a shorter term. That's it. Everything else is decoration.

Here's why the headline percentages are so misleading. VPN providers list a "monthly" price of around $12.99. Almost nobody pays that. The 1-year plan usually runs $4.99–$6.99 per month, and the 2-year plan drops to roughly $2.99. So when NordVPN advertises "80% off," it's comparing the 2-year rate to the monthly rate — technically true, but it's not the comparison a real buyer is making.

The comparison you're actually making is 2-year versus 1-year. And on that basis, the Black Friday discount is usually worth about $2 to $3 per month, or $50 to $80 across the full term. Still a good deal. Just not the 80% the banner promises.

How to Tell a Real VPN Discount From Marketing Noise

Before you click anything, run through this checklist. It takes two minutes and will save you from the two most common traps.

Check the renewal price. This is the big one. A plan advertised at $2.99/month for the first term often renews at $99–$139 per year. That's a jump from roughly $36/year to $100+/year. Set a calendar reminder for two weeks before renewal so you can decide with a clear head rather than getting auto-charged.

Check the guarantee length. Thirty days is the industry standard and it's genuinely useful — you can test speeds, streaming access, and app quality on your own connection. Anything under 14 days is a warning sign. A few providers (Surfshark, PIA) have offered extended windows during past Black Friday events.

Count the "free months" honestly. Three extra months on a 24-month plan is a 12.5% bonus. Nice, but it's not the reason to buy. Don't let it tip a decision you were already unsure about.

Look at what's bundled. NordVPN's higher tiers include a password manager and encrypted cloud storage. If you're already paying for those separately, the bundle changes the math. If you're not, don't let it inflate your decision.

Skip anything with "lifetime" in the title. More on this below, but the short version: VPN infrastructure costs money every month. A company selling you 50 years of access for $39 is either going to disappear or degrade. Both outcomes cost you more than the subscription you avoided.

The Deals Worth Looking At in 2026

NordVPN Black Friday

NordVPN is the provider most people end up with, and the Black Friday pricing is usually the best it offers all year. The 2-year plan typically lands around $2.99–$3.99 per month depending on tier, with three extra months thrown in. The Basic tier covers the VPN itself. Plus adds the password manager and cross-platform threat protection. Complete adds 1TB of encrypted cloud storage.

The reason NordVPN keeps winning comparisons isn't marketing spend — it's that the core product holds up. NordLynx (their WireGuard implementation) delivers some of the fastest speeds we've measured, the server network is over 6,000 across 110+ countries, and the obfuscated servers work in networks that actively block VPN traffic. Ten simultaneous devices is enough for most households.

The honest downside: the app has gotten busier over the years, and the tiered pricing means the cheapest plan isn't the one the ads show. Also, the 2-year commitment is a real commitment. If you're not sure you'll still want a VPN in 2028, the 1-year plan at a slightly higher monthly rate is the safer call.

ExpressVPN Deal

ExpressVPN almost never competes on price, and Black Friday doesn't change that. Expect around $4.99–$6.67 per month on a 2-year plan with 3–4 months free — roughly $30 to $50 more over the term than NordVPN or Surfshark.

What you're paying for is consistency. The Lightway protocol is genuinely excellent for reconnecting quickly after sleep or network changes, which matters if you use a VPN on a laptop that moves between Wi-Fi networks all day. The router app is the easiest to set up of any major provider, and if you're covering a whole home at the router level, that saves real time. Streaming unblocking is dependable across Netflix, BBC iPlayer, and the usual suspects.

The downside is the price, and the fact that eight devices is now on the low side. If you're covering a family of five with phones, laptops, and tablets, you'll hit that limit. ExpressVPN also has a thinner feature set than NordVPN at a higher price — no bundled password manager, no cloud storage.

Surfshark Black Friday

Surfshark's pitch is simple: unlimited devices on one account, at the lowest per-month price of any major provider. Black Friday usually pushes the 2-year plan to around $1.99–$2.49 per month, which works out to under $60 for the full term.

If you have a large household, or you're the person everyone asks to sort out their tech, unlimited connections genuinely matters. Most providers cap at 5–10 devices, and hitting that cap mid-year is annoying. Surfshark also includes a decent ad blocker and a separate IP-rotating feature that's useful for certain research and scraping tasks.

Where it falls short: speeds are good but not quite at NordVPN's level, the server network is smaller, and the streaming unblocking is less reliable — it works most of the time, but "most of the time" gets frustrating if Netflix is your main reason for subscribing. Customer support is chat-only.
