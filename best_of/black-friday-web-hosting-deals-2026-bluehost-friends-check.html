Black Friday Web Hosting Deals 2026: Bluehost and Friends — What to Check Before You Buy

Every November, the same thing happens. Hosting companies slash their prices by 70%, 80%, sometimes 90%, and the internet fills up with "lowest price ever" banners. Then January rolls around and half those buyers discover they're locked into a three-year contract at a rate they didn't fully read.

Black Friday is genuinely the best time of year to buy hosting. It's also the easiest time to Make an expensive mistake, because the discount is designed to distract you from the number that actually matters: the renewal price.

I've spent the last few weeks pulling apart the 2026 offers from Bluehost, Hostinger, SiteGround, DreamHost, and A2 Hosting. Below is what each deal actually gets you, where the fine print bites, and which one I'd put my own money on this year.

The one thing nobody puts in the banner ad

Web hosting uses a two-tier pricing model, and it's not going away. You pay a low introductory rate for your first term — usually 12, 36, or 48 months — then you renew at the standard rate, which is often three to four times higher.

Bluehost's Basic plan is the classic example. During Black Friday you'll see it advertised somewhere around $2.75/month. That's real. What's less prominent is that it renews at roughly $10.99/month once your initial term ends. On a three-year term that's a jump of about $300 over the following three years.

This isn't a scam — it's how the entire shared hosting industry funds its marketing. But it does mean the "deal" you're comparing isn't the promo price. It's the average cost per month across your whole intended stay.

So before you click anything, do this: find the renewal rate, multiply it by the number of years you actually plan to keep the site, add the promo term, and divide by the total months. That number is your real price. It's usually still good on Black Friday. It's just not the number on the banner.

What actually matters in a hosting deal

Price is the headline, but four other things decide whether you'll be happy in month eight.

Storage type and limits. "Unlimited storage" almost always comes with an inode cap buried in the terms. Bluehost's Basic plan gives you 10 GB of SSD storage, which is fine for a brochure site and cramped for anything with a media library. Hostinger's Premium plan gives you 100 GB. Read the actual number.

Free domain and its renewal cost. Most hosts throw in a free domain for year one. Year two is typically $15–$20. Bluehost includes a free domain for the first year on all shared plans; DreamHost does too, plus free Whois privacy, which most others charge for.

Backups. Daily automated backups are not standard everywhere. Hostinger includes them on Business; SiteGround includes them on all plans but limits retention; Bluehost's Basic plan doesn't include automatic daily backups at the base tier. If you're running a store, this matters more than any discount.

Support channels and hours. 24/7 phone support sounds identical across providers until you actually need it at 11pm. Bluehost has phone and chat around the clock. SiteGround's support is consistently rated the best in the budget tier, and it shows in the response times. Hostinger is chat-only on lower plans.

Performance. Shared hosting is shared hosting, but there's a real spread. A2 Hosting's Turbo plans and SiteGround's newer infrastructure both benchmark noticeably faster than Bluehost's standard shared tier. If your site is image-heavy or you're running WooCommerce, this is worth paying a little more for.

Provider-by-provider: the honest version

Bluehost

The Bluehost Black Friday offer is usually the most heavily promoted of the bunch, and it's a legitimate entry point for a first website. The WordPress integration is tighter than most — Bluehost is an official WordPress.org recommended host, and the one-click install plus pre-installed SSL makes the setup genuinely quick for someone who's never built a site.

Pros:

  • Free domain for the first year on all shared plans
  • 24/7 phone support, which most budget hosts don't offer
  • Deep WordPress integration and a beginner-friendly dashboard
  • Black Friday discounts are among the largest in raw percentage terms

Cons:

  • Renewal prices are high relative to competitors
  • Basic plan's 10 GB storage is tight
  • No automatic daily backups at the entry tier
  • Performance is average — not the host to pick if speed is your priority

SiteGround's Black Friday discount is real, but you need to go in knowing that the renewal rate is roughly six times the promo rate. What you're paying for is support and infrastructure, and in my experience it's the best in this price bracket. Their support team actually solves problems rather than reading from a script, and the newer data center setup is noticeably quicker than Bluehost's standard shared tier.

A2's Turbo plans are the speed play in this roundup. Their Runway plan is the budget option and it's fine, but the real Black Friday value is usually on the Turbo tier, which benchmarks meaningfully faster than standard shared hosting. Worth a look if you're running WooCommerce or anything with heavy assets.

The pre-purchase checklist

Before you enter card details on any Black Friday hosting sale, run through these seven items. It takes five minutes and saves you real money.

  • Find the renewal price. It's usually in the cart summary or a linked pricing page, not the banner.
  • Check the term you're committing to. 48 months is a long time if the host disappoints you in month six.
  • Confirm the refund window. 30 days is standard; DreamHost's 97 days is the outlier.
  • Note what happens to your free domain. If you leave before renewal, you may lose it or pay a transfer fee.
  • Check backup policy. Daily automated backups should be non-negotiable if you're running a store.
  • Test the support before you need it. Open a chat with a pre-sales question. The response quality tells you a lot.
  • Watch the checkout upsells. Domain privacy, SiteLock, and premium backups are often pre-checked. Uncheck what you don't need.
