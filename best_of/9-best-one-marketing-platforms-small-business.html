9 Best All-in-One Marketing Platforms for Small Business in 2026

Most small businesses don't need fifteen tools. They need one place to send email, build a landing page, track leads, and see what's actually working. The problem is that "all-in-one" has become a marketing word that means almost nothing — plenty of platforms claim it while charging you extra for basic things like automation or a second user.

So we signed up for real accounts on nine platforms, ran the same test campaign through each one (a welcome sequence, a landing page, a lead-scoring setup, and a simple abandoned-cart flow where supported), and tracked what broke. Here's what we found, including the places where the popular choice is genuinely the wrong one for a small team.

A quick note on pricing: figures below are list prices checked in early 2026, mostly billed annually. Almost every vendor discounts for annual prepayment and raises prices at contact-count thresholds, so treat these as starting points rather than final numbers.

The Short List

If you're in a hurry:

  • Best overall for most small businesses: HubSpot Marketing Hub — free tier is genuinely usable, and it scales without a rebuild.
  • Best automation for the money: ActiveCampaign — the most flexible builder we tested under $100/month.
  • Best free plan: Brevo — 300 emails a day, forever, with a real CRM attached.
  • Best for ecommerce: Klaviyo — if you sell products online, nothing else comes close on revenue attribution.
  • Best budget all-in-one: Systeme.io — funnels, courses, email, and affiliates for $27/month.

Now the details.

1. HubSpot Marketing Hub

Best for: Businesses that expect to grow and don't want to migrate platforms in two years.

HubSpot is the default recommendation for a reason, and it's not the marketing. It's the fact that the free CRM is excellent and the marketing tools sit on top of the same contact record. When someone fills out a form, that activity shows up on the contact timeline next to their support tickets and deals. You stop stitching together spreadsheets.

The free tier includes forms, email (2,000 sends/month), a basic landing page builder, and live chat. The Starter tier at around $20/month adds automation, A/B testing, and removes HubSpot branding. The jump to Professional — roughly $890/month — is where most small businesses get priced out. That tier adds proper multi-touch automation, custom reporting, and lead scoring.

Pros:

  • Free CRM is better than most paid CRMs
  • Contact timeline unifies marketing, sales, and support activity
  • Documentation and community are unusually good
  • No migration needed as you grow

Cons:

  • Professional tier is a brutal price jump
  • Contact-based pricing punishes list growth
  • Marketing emails count against a monthly send cap even on paid plans
  • The page builder is fine, not great

2. ActiveCampaign

Best for: Businesses whose marketing depends on behavior-triggered sequences.

ActiveCampaign's automation builder is the best we used in this price range. You can branch on email opens, site visits, custom fields, purchase history, and lead score — and the visual builder doesn't fall apart once a flow gets past about fifteen steps. Most competitors cap you at simple if/then logic or charge extra for it.

Email deliverability was consistently solid in our tests. The built-in CRM is lightweight but usable for a small sales team, and the split automations (run two versions of a flow and compare revenue) are genuinely useful for optimization.

The weak spots are real, though. The email designer feels a decade old. There's no native social media scheduling — you'll need a separate tool. And the contact tiers are steep: 2,500 contacts on the Plus plan runs close to $100/month.

Pros:

  • Deepest automation logic under $100/month
  • Built-in lead scoring and CRM
  • Split automation testing
  • Reliable deliverability

Cons:

  • Dated email and page builder
  • No social scheduling
  • Price climbs quickly with list size
  • Onboarding requires real time investment

3. Brevo

Best for: Small businesses that want a free tier they can actually run on.

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) is the most generous free plan in this category. You get 300 emails per day, unlimited contacts, a CRM, SMS credits, and a basic automation builder — not a 14-day trial, not a 500-contact ceiling. For a business sending a weekly newsletter to a few thousand people, that's a real, ongoing free option.

Paid plans start around $9/month for the Starter tier. The automation builder is simpler than ActiveCampaign's — think linear sequences with basic branching — but it handles welcome series, birthday flows, and re-engagement campaigns without trouble. Brevo also bundles transactional email and SMS, which is handy if you're sending order confirmations.

The tradeoff: templates are plain, the visual builder is basic, and the platform doesn't do much beyond email, SMS, and CRM. If you need landing pages or social scheduling, you're adding tools back in.

Pros:

  • Best free tier in the category
  • Unlimited contacts even on free
  • SMS and transactional email included
  • Cheap at every tier

Cons:

  • Basic automation compared to ActiveCampaign
  • Plain templates
  • Deliverability was slightly less consistent in our tests
  • Not a true all-in-one for pages and social

4. GetResponse

Best for: Course creators and coaches running webinars.

GetResponse has quietly become a webinar platform with email attached. The built-in webinar tool handles registration pages, reminder sequences, live rooms, and replay follow-ups — and the automation triggers connect webinar attendance to your email flows. If you sell through live events or evergreen webinars, that's a combination you'd otherwise pay two or three subscriptions for.

The free plan covers 500 contacts with limited automation. Paid email marketing starts around $19/month, but the automation and webinar features you probably want land in the $59–$99/month range. There's also a decent landing page builder and a course-hosting add-on.

Honest caveat: the interface feels older than it should in 2026, and the automation builder, while capable, is clunkier than ActiveCampaign's. The contact-based pricing also gets expensive past 10,000 contacts.

Pros:

  • Webinars built in, with automation triggers
  • Landing pages and course hosting available
  • Free plan for small lists
  • Good template library

Cons:

  • Dated interface
  • Automation builder is less flexible than rivals
  • Costs climb fast above 10,000 contacts
  • Some features gated behind higher tiers

5. Klaviyo

Best for: Ecommerce stores on Shopify, WooCommerce, or BigCommerce.

Klaviyo is not a general-purpose marketing platform — it's an ecommerce revenue machine. Its flows (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase, win-back) come pre-built and pre-populated with product data pulled straight from your store. The revenue attribution is the part that matters: you can see exactly how much each flow and each campaign generated, not just how many clicks it got.

The free tier covers 250 contacts and 500 email sends per month. Paid plans start around $45/month at 1,000 contacts and scale with both contact count and send volume. That's more expensive than Mailchimp at the same list size — but if you're running an online store, the attribution alone usually justifies it.

If you don't sell products, skip Klaviyo. Its strengths are wasted on a service business, and its pricing model (based on both contacts and sends) gets punishing for large, low-conversion lists.

Pros:

  • Revenue attribution per flow and campaign
  • Deep native integrations with major store platforms
  • Strong segmentation using purchase history
  • SMS marketing included

Cons:

  • Expensive for non-ecommerce use
  • Pricing scales on contacts and sends
  • Steeper learning curve than Mailchimp
  • Overkill for simple newsletters

6. Systeme.io

Best for: Solopreneurs launching a first offer on a tight budget.

Systeme.io packs funnels, landing pages, email marketing, course hosting, an affiliate program manager, and a blog into one subscription — and the free plan covers 2,000 contacts and three funnels. The Startup tier at $27/month is one of the cheapest ways to get a complete sales and marketing stack running.

The funnel builder is genuinely good for the price. You can build a tripwire sequence, an upsell path, and an affiliate program without touching another tool. For a first product launch, that's everything you need.

What you give up: depth. The email automation is basic compared to ActiveCampaign. There's no real CRM for a sales team. Deliverability support is limited, and customer support is email-only with slower response times than the bigger players. It's a starter platform — most businesses outgrow it within a year or two.

Pros:

  • Free plan with 2,000 contacts
  • Funnels, courses, and affiliates in one place
  • $27/month for a full stack
  • Good for first launches

Cons:

  • Shallow automation
  • No proper CRM
  • Email-only support
  • Businesses tend to outgrow it

7. Keap (formerly Infusionsoft)

Best for: Service businesses that need marketing and sales follow-up in one system.

Keap was built for the business that sells a service and loses leads to slow follow-up. Its strength is automating the sales process — quote follow-ups, appointment reminders, pipeline stages — alongside email marketing. If you're a consultant, agency, or local service business with a sales pipeline, that combination is more useful than another email blaster.
