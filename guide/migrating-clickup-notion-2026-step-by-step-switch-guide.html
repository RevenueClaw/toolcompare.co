What you're really trading

ClickUp is a project management tool with documents bolted on. Notion is a document tool with databases bolted on. That single sentence explains 80% of the friction people hit mid-migration.

ClickUp ships with native time tracking, task dependencies, recurring tasks, goals, chat, and an automation engine that fires on a long list of triggers. Notion gives you databases (tables, boards, calendars, timelines, galleries), relations and rollups between them, page-level permissions, and a writing surface that doesn't feel like a form. It does not give you native time tracking, and its recurring-task options are thinner.

Pros and cons, no marketing gloss

ClickUp pros: time tracking without an add-on; automations that handle real conditional logic; Gantt and workload views out of the box; predictable per-seat pricing with no renewal jump. ClickUp cons: interface density that new hires find exhausting; notification noise; performance on very large workspaces; per-seat cost that scales fast once you pass 20 people.

Notion pros: documentation and project data live in the same place, so specs, meeting notes, and tasks stop living in three tools; relations and rollups handle reporting ClickUp can't without custom fields; page permissions are more granular for client-facing work. Notion cons: no native time tracking; automations are thinner than ClickUp's; large databases get slow; and the blank canvas means bad workspace architecture is easy to create and painful to fix later.

FAQ

Q: Can Notion import my ClickUp tasks directly?

A: Notion imports CSV, so export ClickUp views/lists as CSV (assignees, statuses, and custom fields included) and map the columns to database properties during import. Automations, dashboards, and Gantt history don't transfer — rebuild those.

Q: What do I lose by moving from ClickUp to Notion?

A: Native project depth: resource/workload management, native time tracking, click-to-run dashboards, and large automation quotas. ClickUp Business carries 10,000 automations per month; Notion automations are more limited and its AI runs on credits. If those features are load-bearing, don't migrate.

Q: How long does a ClickUp-to-Notion migration take for a small team?

A: Plan on one focused week: a day to export and restructure, two days rebuilding databases and templates, a day for integrations and permissions, and the rest as a paid overlap month while the team relearns where things live.
