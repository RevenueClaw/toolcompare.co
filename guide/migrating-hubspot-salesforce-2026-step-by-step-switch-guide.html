Who this switch actually makes sense for

A migration is a six-to-eight week project with a real cost in lost selling time. Salesforce earns that cost when you need things HubSpot doesn't do well: custom objects, granular role hierarchies, territory management, deep reporting on cross-object data, or a sales process with approvals and multi-step deal governance.

It does not earn that cost if the honest reason is "HubSpot got expensive." HubSpot's pricing climbs because it bundles marketing email, landing pages, forms, ads, and blog hosting into the same seat. Salesforce Starter Suite is cheap per seat, but it does not replace those things. You'd be buying Salesforce and a marketing tool. That's usually more money, not less.

Quick gut check: if more than a third of your HubSpot usage is marketing email, forms, or landing pages, the math gets ugly fast. If almost all of it is deals, contacts, and pipeline, the switch is cleaner than most people expect.

FAQ

Q: Can I export HubSpot contacts, deals, and emails to Salesforce?

A: Contacts and deals export as CSV and map into Salesforce's import wizards. Email threads and activity history need the HubSpot export tools; property change history and workflow logic generally don't survive and must be re-created.

Q: Should I migrate to Salesforce Core or Starter Suite?

A: Starter Suite ($25/user/mo) fits teams of roughly 1-10 sellers who need the standard lead-to-close pipeline. Core ($195/user/mo, billed annually) is for teams that need Agentforce, Slack, and Tableau built in. Middle-ground customization needs usually land on Pro Suite ($100/user/mo).

Q: How do I avoid double-paying during the migration?

A: Run Salesforce Starter Suite in parallel with HubSpot Free (the free CRM keeps working while you pay for nothing new), reconcile one pipeline, then flip to paid Salesforce tiers only after your first clean month-end close. HubSpot contracts are monthly per seat, so a one-month overlap is cheap insurance.
