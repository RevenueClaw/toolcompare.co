Migrating from Grammarly to ProWritingAid in 2026: The Step-by-Step Switch Guide with Real 2026 Prices

Switching writing tools is a lot like changing banks. The actual move takes an afternoon, but the fear of losing something — saved words, a custom dictionary, a workflow your team finally got used to — keeps people on the wrong side of the decision for years. Most Grammarly-to-ProWritingAid switchers aren't chasing a discount. They're chasing the report-level feedback Grammarly doesn't really do: pacing, readability, overused words, sticky sentences, repeated phrasing across a 90,000-word manuscript.

For the feature-by-feature head-to-head, start with our full Grammarly vs ProWritingAid comparison, then come back here for the migration playbook.

Below is the exact order of operations we'd use if we were moving a team of five tomorrow, plus what each step costs and where people usually trip.

What actually changes day to day

Both tools sit in the same places — browser extension, Word, Google Docs — so the surface looks familiar. The feedback underneath is different enough that the first week feels like a demotion if nobody warns you.

Grammar and correctness

Grammarly is still the stronger line editor on ordinary prose. It catches more of the messy, real-world sentence errors (agreement, misplaced modifiers, awkward phrasing) and its full-sentence rewrites are more usable straight out of the box. ProWritingAid's grammar checker has improved a lot, but it's more literal. It will flag things Grammarly would quietly fix and it will occasionally miss a clunky construction a human editor would catch.

Practical implication: expect more false positives in week one. Don't turn off the Grammar report — train yourself to skim it rather than accept every suggestion.

Style, readability, and long-form structure

This is where the switch pays off. ProWritingAid runs a library of more than 20 reports on a single document: Style, Clarity, Sticky Sentences, Overused Words, Repeated Words (Echoes), Sentence Length variation, Pacing, Readability, Dialogue, Diction, Cliches, Transitions, Pronoun use, Homonyms. Grammarly gives you a clarity score and some tone signals, but it doesn't hand you a diagnostic breakdown you can work through chapter by chapter.

If you write novels, long sales pages, or anything over 3,000 words, that difference is the whole argument for moving.

AI writing assistance

Grammarly's AI layer is baked into the same panel — prompts for rewriting, shortening, changing tone, generating drafts. The free tier caps you at 100 AI prompts a month; Pro raises that to 2,000.

FAQ

Q: Can ProWritingAid import my Grammarly custom dictionary?

A: There's no direct importer, but Grammarly exports your personal dictionary as plain text; paste the entries into ProWritingAid's custom dictionary list and you're done in minutes.

Q: Do Grammarly add-ins work the same on ProWritingAid?

A: Coverage differs. Grammarly rides a system-wide layer (browser, desktop, Office, mobile Keyboard). ProWritingAid integrates with Word, Google Docs, Scrivener, and browsers, but its browser coverage is narrower — check your specific writing surface on its site before you cancel.

Q: Is ProWritingAid better for long-form writing?

A: Generally yes — chapter-level reports, style-overviews, and manuscript analytics are built for books and essays, where Grammarly's strengths remain quick surface corrections and tone rewrites. Match the tool to the length of what you write.
